In India, third-party car insurance is compulsory by law under the Motor Vehicles Act, and it pays for harm you cause to other people and their property. A comprehensive policy adds own damage cover for your own car, and add-ons such as zero depreciation and roadside assistance extend it further.

This is a plain guide to how car insurance works in India, written for new drivers and first-time car owners, as of November 2026. We do not quote premiums or recommend any insurer. Prices depend on your car, city, age and claim history, and the policy wording, not a summary like this, decides what is covered. The insurance regulator, IRDAI, publishes the rules insurers must follow.

Is car insurance mandatory in India?

Yes, third-party insurance is mandatory for every motor vehicle used in a public place. Section 146 of the Motor Vehicles Act, 1988 makes it an offence to drive, or let someone drive, a vehicle without at least third-party cover. Driving uninsured can lead to a fine and other penalties, and leaves you personally liable for any harm you cause.

  • New cars come with long-term third-party cover. Following a Supreme Court order, new cars sold from 1 September 2018 must have three years of third-party cover, and new two-wheelers five years.
  • Own damage cover is optional by law, although banks and finance companies usually insist on it for a car bought on a loan.
  • Your policy is on the vehicle record. Police can check whether a car is insured against its registration number. Our Hyderabad e-challan guide explains how challans reach you.

What are third-party, own damage and comprehensive cover?

They are three building blocks: third-party covers others, own damage covers your car, and comprehensive is the two together.

TypeWhat it coversWho needs it
Third-party (liability)Injury or death of other people, and damage to their property, caused by your carEveryone, by law
Standalone own damageDamage to your own car from accidents, theft, fire, floods and other listed risksOwners who already have a separate third-party policy, such as a new car's long-term cover
ComprehensiveThird-party and own damage in one policyMost owners who want their own car protected

Standalone own damage policies have been available since 2019, so the owner of a new car with three-year third-party cover can renew just the own damage part each year. Policies also include compulsory personal accident cover for the owner-driver, unless you already have equivalent cover. Every policy has exclusions: claims are commonly refused for driving without a valid licence, drink driving, normal wear and tear and mechanical breakdown. Read that section before you buy.

Your licence matters to your insurance. A claim can be refused if the driver had no valid licence at the time, and there is no grace period after a licence expires. See our guide to driving licence renewal in Telangana.

Which add-ons are worth knowing about?

Add-ons are optional extras on a comprehensive or own damage policy, each for an extra premium. The common ones:

  • Zero depreciation (nil depreciation). A normal claim deducts depreciation on replaced parts such as plastic, rubber and fibre, so you pay part of the bill. This add-on removes most of that deduction. It is popular for newer cars, and insurers usually limit it by car age and number of claims.
  • Roadside assistance. Help if you break down: towing, a flat battery, a flat tyre, fuel delivery or a locked-out car. Useful on long highway drives.
  • Engine protection. Covers engine damage from water entering the engine or oil leaks, which a standard policy often excludes. Worth thinking about in a city where underpasses flood in the monsoon.
  • Return to invoice. If the car is stolen or written off, pays the gap between the insured value and the original invoice price.
  • NCB protection. Lets you make a limited number of claims without losing your no claim bonus.
  • Consumables. Pays for items such as engine oil, nuts and bolts and coolant used in a repair.

Choose add-ons for how and where you drive rather than buying all of them.

How does the no claim bonus work?

The no claim bonus (NCB) is a discount on the own damage premium for every claim-free year. It does not apply to the third-party part. The standard steps are:

Claim-free years in a rowNCB on own damage premium
120%
225%
335%
445%
5 or more50%
  • A claim resets it, usually to zero at the next renewal, unless you have NCB protection.
  • It belongs to you, not the car. When you sell a car and buy another, ask the insurer to transfer the NCB to the new policy.
  • Renew on time. If your policy stays lapsed for more than 90 days, the NCB is generally lost, and driving even one day without insurance is an offence.
  • Small damage: for a minor scrape, compare the repair bill with the NCB you would lose before claiming.

How do you make a car insurance claim?

Tell your insurer as soon as possible, follow its steps, and keep records of everything. A typical own damage claim works like this:

  1. Make the scene safe first. Get help for anyone hurt and move out of traffic if you can. Our guide on what to do after a car accident in Hyderabad covers the first few minutes.
  2. Take photos of the damage, the scene, number plates and any documents exchanged.
  3. Inform the insurer through its helpline or app, and note the claim number.
  4. Report to the police when someone is injured or killed, when another party's property is involved, or when the car is stolen. The insurer will ask for the FIR or report.
  5. Survey. The insurer appoints a surveyor to assess the damage. Under IRDAI rules from 2024, surveyors must be appointed quickly, and claims are to be decided within set time limits after the survey report.
  6. Repair. At a network garage, a cashless claim means the insurer pays the garage directly, minus deductibles and depreciation. Elsewhere you pay and claim reimbursement.

Claims against another driver for injury or death are a separate legal process, usually through the Motor Accident Claims Tribunal, and the third-party insurer handles them. If you are unhappy with how a claim is handled, complain to the insurer first, then use IRDAI's Bima Bharosa grievance system or the Insurance Ombudsman.

Which documents should you carry?

Carry your driving licence, registration certificate, insurance certificate and pollution under control (PUC) certificate, either as paper or in the official apps.

  • DigiLocker and mParivahan count. Under the Central Motor Vehicles Rules, as amended in 2018, and a Ministry of Road Transport and Highways advisory, documents in these two apps are treated as equal to the originals. Police can also verify your insurance against the vehicle record.
  • A photo or PDF in your gallery or email does not have the same legal standing, so add your documents to DigiLocker or mParivahan.
  • Keep the insurer's helpline and your policy number saved in your phone, and in the car.

Can safer driving keep insurance simpler?

Yes, in the most practical way: fewer claims mean you keep your no claim bonus and avoid the paperwork. Most small city claims come from scrapes in tight lanes, misjudged gaps, parking knocks and rear-end bumps in slow traffic.
