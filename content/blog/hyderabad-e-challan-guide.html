An e-challan is a traffic fine issued electronically against a vehicle's registration number, usually from a camera photo, and in Hyderabad you check, pay or dispute it on the official Telangana Police e-challan site, echallan.tspolice.gov.in. Search by your vehicle number, check the photo, and either pay online or raise a grievance if it is wrong.

This guide covers how challans are raised in Hyderabad, Cyberabad and the other Telangana police areas, how to look them up, how to pay, what happens if you ignore them and how to dispute a wrong one, as of November 2026. We have left out fine amounts on purpose: they are set by law and change, and the e-challan site shows the exact amount for each challan.

What is an e-challan?

An e-challan is an electronic notice of a traffic offence, linked to the vehicle's registration number and sent to the registered owner. It replaces the old paper challan written at the roadside, although officers can still issue challans on the spot.

Each challan records the date, time and place, the offence, the amount and, for camera challans, a photo of the vehicle. Hyderabad Traffic Police, Cyberabad, Rachakonda (Malkajgiri) and the other commissionerates in Telangana all feed into the same integrated e-challan system, so one search shows challans from across the state.

Key facts

  • Official site: echallan.tspolice.gov.in, the Telangana Police integrated e-challan system.
  • Who it is issued to: the registered owner of the vehicle, whoever was driving.
  • How to search: vehicle number, driving licence number, challan number or mobile number.
  • How to pay: UPI, debit card, credit card or net banking on the official site.
  • Disputes: a grievance option on the same site, with reasons such as "Not my vehicle", "Wrong challan" and "Double challan".
  • Official SMS: the site says genuine e-challan messages come only from a sender header starting "ECHALN".

How do cameras and police issue e-challans?

Most challans in Hyderabad now come from cameras, not from an officer stopping you. There are three main sources.

  • Junction and roadside cameras. Cameras at signals and along main roads capture offences such as jumping a red light, crossing the stop line, riding without a helmet and driving on the wrong side.
  • Officers with handheld cameras. Traffic police photograph offences such as wrong parking, a rider without a helmet, triple riding or a phone in hand, and the photo becomes a challan.
  • Spot challans. At checkpoints an officer issues a challan with a handheld device, and you can pay there or later. The official site notes a small user charge on spot challans.

Because the challan follows the number plate, a dirty, bent or non-standard plate can lead to wrong readings, and a car sold without transferring the RC can keep collecting challans in the old owner's name. How do you check an e-challan by vehicle number?

Go to the official e-challan site, enter your vehicle number and the captcha, and it lists every pending challan against that vehicle. Step by step:

  1. Open echallan.tspolice.gov.in in your browser. Type the address yourself rather than tapping a link in a message.
  2. Choose the vehicle number search and enter the registration number in full, for example TG 09 followed by the series and number. Both older TS and newer TG plates are searched by their own number.
  3. Enter the captcha and submit.
  4. Read each challan: date, time, place, offence and amount. Open the photo and check that the vehicle and number plate really are yours.

The same site lets you search by licence number, challan number or mobile number, look up payment receipts and check the penalty points on a driving licence. The national Parivahan e-challan site also shows challans for many states.

Check your vehicle every month or two, and always before you sell it or buy a used one. A challan you never knew about is much easier to deal with early.

How do you pay an e-challan, and what if you do not?

Pay on the official e-challan site by UPI, debit card, credit card or net banking. Select the challans you want to clear, pay, and note the transaction reference number shown at the end. You can download a receipt later from the payment receipt search.

  • If money is debited but the challan still shows as pending, the site advises waiting 24 hours. If it is still not updated, contact the helpdesk for your commissionerate with the reference number. The site says failed payments are generally refunded within seven working days.
  • Use only official channels. Telangana Police warn about fake e-challan websites and apps that copy the real one. Never pay through a link that arrives by SMS, WhatsApp or email.
  • Helplines. As of November 2026 the official site lists helpdesk numbers for each commissionerate, including Hyderabad on 040-27852721 and 040-27852772.
A message saying "your challan is pending, pay now or face court action" with a link is a common scam. Check the challan yourself on the official site before paying anything.

What happens if you do not pay?

Unpaid challans do not go away, and recent national rules make ignoring them more costly. In January 2026 the Ministry of Road Transport and Highways amended the Central Motor Vehicles Rules on challans and licences. As widely reported, the changes include:

  • A window to act. A set period, reported as 45 days from issue, to either pay the challan or contest it online with evidence. A challan you neither pay nor contest is treated as accepted.
  • Blocked services. A vehicle with unpaid challans can be flagged on the national portal, which stops services such as transfer of ownership, change of address and other RTA applications, apart from paying road tax.
  • Licence disqualification. The Central Motor Vehicles (Third Amendment) Rules, 2026 (G.S.R. 48(E), 21 January 2026) let the licensing authority disqualify a driver for five or more offences within one year, counted from 1 January 2026.

Police can also stop vehicles with many pending challans during special drives. If you are unsure which rules apply to your challan, the official site and the Ministry of Road Transport and Highways publish the current position.

How do you dispute a wrong e-challan?

Raise a grievance on the official e-challan site with evidence, as soon as you notice the mistake. Wrong challans usually come from a misread or fake number plate, a vehicle you have already sold, a duplicate of an earlier challan, or a photo that does not show the offence.

  1. Find and open the challan on the official site and look carefully at the photo.
  2. Select the grievance option and choose the reason that fits: not my vehicle, wrong challan or double challan.
  3. Add clear remarks, for example "The photo shows a white hatchback; my vehicle is a grey sedan" or "Vehicle sold on this date; RC transferred".
  4. Keep your evidence ready: RC, a photo of your own number plate, the sale and transfer papers, or proof of where the vehicle was at that time.
  5. Note the reference and watch for the SMS update on the outcome.

If someone is using your number on another vehicle, the site has a separate fake number plate complaint, and a sold out vehicle option for vehicles you no longer own. Do not simply pay a challan you believe is wrong; once paid, it is treated as settled.

Have there been challan discount schemes?

Yes, but only as one-off schemes, and none was running as of November 2026. Treat this as history, not a promise of another:

WhenWhat happened
March to April 2022A one-time discount on pending challans, started on 1 March 2022 and extended into mid-April 2022.
26 December 2023 to 15 February 2024A government order gave discounts on pending challans, with different rates by vehicle type. It was extended twice and ended on 15 February 2024.
December 2024A viral message claimed a new discount; Hyderabad Traffic Police said it was fake.

Fake discount messages appear regularly, and they are often a lure to a payment link. A genuine scheme is announced by Telangana Police and the state government and shows on the official site itself.

Can you practise the habits that avoid challans?

The best way to deal with challans is not to collect them. Most camera challans come from a few habits: stopping behind the stop line, not jumping an amber light, keeping to your lane and parking only where it is allowed. Our Hyderabad parking guide covers the last one.
