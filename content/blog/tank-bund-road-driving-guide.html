Tank Bund Road is one of the easiest main roads in Hyderabad to learn on: wide, divided and nearly straight, with Hussain Sagar on one side the whole way. What makes driving on Tank Bund Road tricky is not the layout but the people on it: two-wheelers on both sides of you, buses pulling in, and walkers crossing to the lake in the evening.

This guide covers what the road is like, when it gets busy, what to look out for and how to rehearse the drive first in Sawari, the free browser driving simulator built on real Hyderabad streets.

What Tank Bund Road is like

Tank Bund Road runs along the top of the old embankment on the eastern shore of Hussain Sagar, the large lake between Hyderabad and Secunderabad that dates back to the Qutb Shahi period. For most drivers it is simply the direct lakeside link between the two cities, and on a clear morning it is one of the nicest stretches of road in town.

Layout and lanes

It is a divided road: a median separates the two directions, and each carriageway is wide enough for about three lanes of cars. Lane markings are there, but at busy times traffic tends to arrange itself into whatever gaps it can find, with scooters and motorbikes filling the spaces between cars. Treat the lane lines as your guide and the traffic around you as the reality.

The road is nearly level, as you would expect on top of a bund. There are no sharp bends and, if you are simply driving its length, no turns to make. You will meet a few signalled junctions near the ends and openings in the median where vehicles cross or turn back.

Landmarks

  • The statues. A long row of statues of Telugu historical and cultural figures stands along the lake side of the road. They are a good reminder of where you are, but they are also a reason people slow down, stop and cross.
  • The Buddha statue. The large monolithic Buddha stands on a rock out in the lake. Driving south, it is out to your right. Enjoy it from the passenger seat; the driver's job is the road.
  • The lake promenade. A walkway runs along the water's edge. In the evening it fills with families, couples and vendors, and many of them cross the road to reach it.

When Tank Bund gets busy

Like most of Hyderabad traffic, Tank Bund follows the working day. Weekday mornings and evenings are the busiest times for through traffic, as people head between Secunderabad and the central offices. Midday on a weekday is usually calmer, and early weekend mornings are often the quietest of all, which makes them a sensible time for a first real drive.

Evenings are a different kind of busy. Even when traffic is moving freely, the lakeside draws crowds after sunset, especially at weekends and on holidays. Expect more people on foot, more vehicles pulling over and more two-wheelers carrying families.

The biggest disruption of the year comes during Ganesh immersion, when idols are taken to Hussain Sagar and Tank Bund is given over to processions, with diversions on the roads around the lake. The road has also been closed to traffic on some evenings for public events. Before you plan a drive at festival time or on a weekend evening, check the advisories from the Hyderabad Traffic Police.

Hazards to watch for when driving on Tank Bund Road

Two-wheelers on both sides

Scooters and motorbikes overtake on the left and the right, and they move between lanes without much warning. Keep a steady line and a steady speed, so they can predict you. Before you change lanes, look over your shoulder as well as in your mirror; a bike can sit in your blind spot for a long time on a road like this. Our guide to sharing the road with autos, buses and two-wheelers goes into more detail.

Buses and autos stopping

Buses pull in to stops on the left and autos stop wherever a passenger waves. If you are in the left lane, expect the vehicle ahead to slow sharply or stop. Leave a bigger gap than feels necessary, and do not swerve right around a stopping bus without checking what is already coming up beside you.

Pedestrians crossing to the lake

People cross Tank Bund away from the crossings, particularly near the statues and in the evening. They often wait in the median and step out as soon as one lane clears. Watch the median as well as the kerb, and slow down where you see groups gathering.

Speed breakers and median openings

Speed breakers on and around the road are not always well painted. Watch for vehicles ahead dipping and braking, and slow down before the hump rather than on it. The openings in the median are where cars, autos and bikes cross or turn back, sometimes slowly and sometimes without signalling. See speed breakers and speed limits in Hyderabad for how to take humps properly.

The view

It sounds obvious, but the lake, the Buddha and the evening lights are distracting. The driver ahead of you may be looking at them too, and slow down for no reason you can see. Keep your eyes on the road and leave the sightseeing to your passengers.

Practical tips for Tank Bund Road

  • Pick a lane early and stay in it. The middle lane is usually the calmest place to settle: away from stopping buses on the left and faster traffic on the right.
  • Keep your speed steady. Follow the posted speed limit signs rather than the fastest car around you. A smooth, predictable speed is safer with so many two-wheelers about.
  • Signal every lane change. Indicate, check, then move. On a wide road it is easy to drift across without thinking.
  • Do not stop to look at the lake. If you want to enjoy the view, park legally where the signs allow and walk.
  • Know where you are leaving. Work out before you start which exit or junction you need at the far end, so you are not changing lanes at the last moment.
  • Use headlights after dusk. Even with street lighting, the crowds and the reflections off the water make the evening harder to read.

Practise Tank Bund Road in Sawari

Sawari's Tank Bund Road route is a 1.7 km southbound drive along the lake, past the statues, with Hussain Sagar on your right. It has no turns to make, so it is the route we suggest for your first drive: you can concentrate on the car and the traffic instead of the directions.

  1. Choose a time and a traffic level. Start with light traffic at midday to learn the car. Then raise the traffic level towards Dense, and try the evening or night lighting to see how the road changes.
  2. Follow the blue route band. It shows your lane on the route. Keep the car inside it and left of the centre line.
  3. Practise your indicators. Use Q for left and E for right before you move across lanes. They cancel themselves after a turn.
  4. Switch views with C. The chase view helps you judge where the car sits in the lane; the driver's seat view is closer to what you will really see. Drag the mouse to look around, which is a good habit to rehearse before lane changes, since the car has no mirrors.
  5. Read your debrief. At the end you get a score and a list of faults with advice: speeding, drifting right of the centre line, taking a speed breaker too fast, collisions with vehicles, pedestrians or the median, running a red light. Your best score on each route is saved in your browser.
If you drift off the route, press R to return to it. When you are comfortable on Tank Bund, try the longer Necklace Road loop on the other side of the lake.

In the simulator vs the real road

The road layout, lane counts, the median and the lake edge in Sawari come from OpenStreetMap, so the shape of the drive is real. Some details are estimated: speed breakers, U-turn openings, roadside stalls, parked vehicles, hoardings, most street lamps and many building facades are placed by rule where the map has no detail. The statues are there as a row, not as faithful likenesses. The speed limit shown in the game comes from map data and may differ from the sign on the day.

So use the simulator to practise habits: lane discipline, steady speed, signalling and watching for people. On the real road, follow the actual signs, signals and traffic police.

Sawari is for practice only. It does not replace lessons with a licensed instructor, and a good score in the game is not a measure of whether you are ready to drive on Hyderabad roads.
