Driving to Hyderabad airport, Rajiv Gandhi International Airport at Shamshabad, is straightforward once you know the three main ways in: the PV Narasimha Rao Expressway from Mehdipatnam, the Outer Ring Road from almost anywhere on the edge of the city, and NH 44 from the south of the old city. The harder parts are judging the time, and knowing the rules for the few minutes you spend at the terminal.

This guide covers the routes, how much time to allow, how drop-off, pick-up and parking work, and what changes when your flight leaves in the middle of the night.

The main ways to drive to Hyderabad airport

The airport sits well south of the city, roughly 20 to 25 km from the centre depending on where you count from. Almost every drive ends on the airport approach road, which has its own speed limit and cameras. Which way you get there depends on where you start.

PV Narasimha Rao Expressway

The PVNR Expressway is the elevated road that starts near the Sarojini Devi Eye Hospital at Mehdipatnam and runs for about 11.6 km to Aramghar, where it comes down to meet NH 44 heading south towards the airport. For anyone in Banjara Hills, Jubilee Hills, Masab Tank, Tolichowki or the central city, it is the usual route, because it lifts you over the busy road and junctions below it.

It is a long, raised road with no shops, no pedestrians and few places to stop. Keep left unless you are overtaking, hold a steady speed, and know which down ramp you need before you reach it. Ramps have been added and changed over the years, so follow the overhead signs rather than memory. Our guide to driving on flyovers in Hyderabad covers joining, leaving and lane choice on raised roads.

Outer Ring Road

The Outer Ring Road is the 158 km, eight-lane expressway round the city, and it has its own exit for Shamshabad and the airport. From Gachibowli, the Financial District, Kokapet, Patancheru, Medchal, Shamirpet or LB Nagar, joining the ORR and following it round is often simpler than crossing town, even when it is a longer distance on paper. It is tolled, so make sure your FASTag is working.

During heavy rain, Cyberabad traffic police have in the past advised airport traffic to take the ORR rather than parts of the older approach roads where water was flowing over the carriageway. Our Outer Ring Road guide explains exits, lane use and tolls.

NH 44 through Aramghar and Shamshabad

NH 44, the old Bengaluru highway, runs south through Aramghar and past Shamshabad town towards the airport turn. If you start in the old city, Bahadurpura or anywhere south of the Musi, you may join it directly. It is a busy national highway with trucks, buses and local traffic, so it is slower than the expressway, but it is the road the expressway itself feeds into.

The airport approach road

Whichever way you come, the final stretch is the airport approach road. Since February 2024 the airport has enforced a limit of 80 km/h along its whole length, with automatic speed enforcement and fines through the traffic police. It is tempting to speed up on a wide, empty road when you are late. Do not; the cameras are there for exactly that.

How much time to allow

Work backwards from the airport's own advice. The airport recommends that you arrive 120 minutes before a domestic departure and 180 minutes before an international one, or whatever your airline tells you. International passengers with valid tickets can enter the terminal only three hours before departure, so there is little point arriving much earlier than that.

Then add:

  • Your driving time at that hour. Check a live traffic app at the time you plan to leave, not the evening before. A drive that takes under an hour late at night can take much longer during the weekday evening peak.
  • A margin for the unexpected. Rain, a breakdown on the expressway or a VIP movement can hold traffic for a long time. If you are flying out on a weekday evening or in heavy rain, be generous.
  • Time to park and walk. If you are leaving the car at the airport, allow time to find a space and walk to departures with your bags.

Before festivals and long weekends, the airport sometimes issues its own advisory asking passengers to come earlier than usual. Look at the official Hyderabad airport website or its social media pages in the days before you fly.

Drop-off, pick-up and the eight-minute rule

Hyderabad airport has a single terminal building for domestic and international flights. Departures and arrivals are on separate levels, each with its own ramp for vehicles.

Dropping someone off

The departures ramp is for dropping passengers, not for waiting. At the time of writing (November 2026), the airport's parking page gives the first eight minutes on the ramp free, with charges after that and vehicles towed if they stay beyond fifteen minutes. The airport also says plainly that vehicles may pull up kerbside only to pick up or drop off passengers, and unattended vehicles at the kerb will be towed and fined.

Eight minutes is enough if you are organised. Have the bags ready to come out, say your goodbyes in the car before you arrive, and pull away as soon as the boot is shut. Two-wheelers and autos are not allowed on the ramps.

Picking someone up

Do not circle the departures level or wait on the arrivals ramp for a flight that has only just landed. A passenger still at baggage reclaim can take a long time to come out. The calmer way is to wait in the car park, ask your passenger to call when they have their bags, then drive round to the arrivals ramp and collect them within the free window.

If you want to meet them inside, the airport has a paid Airport Village area outside the arrivals hall for people meeting passengers.

The ramps take payment by FASTag. A working FASTag on your car makes the barriers quicker, both at the ramps and at the car park.

Parking at the airport

If you are leaving the car while you travel, or you need longer than a few minutes, use the airport car park rather than the ramps. The airport runs a car park with an automated entry and exit system, dedicated FASTag lanes and a valet option. Charges go up with the time you stay and are listed on the airport's parking page; rates change, so check them there before a long trip rather than relying on old figures.

Keep your parking ticket safe. The airport charges a separate fee for a lost ticket on top of normal parking. Take a photo of your bay number before you walk off, so you are not hunting for the car when you come back tired from a flight.

Night flights and early departures

Many flights from Hyderabad leave late at night or before dawn, and that changes the drive.

  • The roads are faster, and so is everyone else. An empty expressway at 2 am invites speed. Stick to the limits, especially on the airport approach road, where the cameras work at night too.
  • Watch for tiredness. If you have been up all day before a late flight, or you are driving home after dropping someone at 4 am, tiredness is the real risk. Open a window, keep the music low and stop somewhere safe if your eyes start to close. Our night driving tips go into this.
  • Expect trucks. NH 44 and the ORR carry heavy goods vehicles through the night. Give them room and do not sit beside them for long.
  • Check for special closures. On some nights, such as New Year's Eve, police have closed the ORR and the PVNR Expressway to cars late at night while still letting airport traffic through. Check Hyderabad and Cyberabad traffic police advisories if you fly on a festival night.

Getting ready with Sawari

Sawari, our free browser driving simulator, does not have the airport, the expressway or the ORR. Its five routes are city streets, and it has no highways and no rain. What it can help with is the city part of the trip: the junctions, two-wheelers and lane changes you meet before you reach the fast roads.

If you start from the Masab Tank side, the Banjara Hills Road No. 1 route is close to where many airport drives begin, with busy junctions and plenty of traffic. The Cyber Towers to Jubilee Hills route covers the IT corridor roads many people drive before they turn towards the ORR. Use them to practise steady lane discipline and signalling, and use the night lighting to get used to reading the road in the dark. Then drive the real route with a calm passenger at a quiet time before you have a flight to catch.

Sawari is for practice only. It does not replace lessons with a licensed instructor. On the real road, follow the signs, the speed limits and any instructions from the traffic police and airport staff.
