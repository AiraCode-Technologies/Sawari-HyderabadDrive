Hyderabad traffic is, on average, lighter than Bengaluru's: in the TomTom Traffic Index for 2025, Bengaluru was the world's second most congested city and Hyderabad was 47th. But both cities jam badly at peak hours around their IT districts, and the bigger difference for a driver is road layout: Hyderabad has a complete 158 km expressway ring road, while Bengaluru has a larger metro.

This is a comparison for people moving between the two cities, not a contest. Both are fast-growing tech cities with similar problems, and both are building their way out of them. Figures are the latest we could verify as of November 2026, with their sources.

Is Hyderabad traffic better than Bengaluru?

On average, yes, by a clear margin. The TomTom Traffic Index for 2025, published in January 2026, ranked Bengaluru second in the world for congestion, behind only Mexico City, with an average congestion level of 74.4%, up 1.7 points on 2024. Hyderabad was 47th, with an average of about 55.5%, a small improvement of 1.3 points.

TomTom also estimated the time a rush-hour commuter loses to congestion in a year: about 168 hours in Bengaluru and about 123 hours in Hyderabad.

Two caveats are worth knowing. First, TomTom measures from connected-car and navigation data, and Bengaluru Traffic Police have said their own data shows travel times close to 10% faster than TomTom reported. Second, a city average hides the roads you actually use. A Hyderabad commute from Kukatpally to the Financial District at 6 pm can feel every bit as slow as a Bengaluru one across Silk Board.

How do the road networks compare?

Hyderabad's biggest advantage is a complete expressway ring; Bengaluru relies more on arterial roads and flyovers.

Hyderabad: the ORR and wide western roads

Hyderabad's Outer Ring Road is a 158 km, eight-lane, access-controlled toll expressway that loops right round the city, linking the IT corridor, the airport and the highways. It lets a lot of traffic avoid the centre altogether. A second, much larger Regional Ring Road of about 340 km is planned but not yet built. Inside the city, the newer western districts have wide roads, many flyovers and long stretches where right turns are replaced by U-turn openings in the median.

Bengaluru: arterial ring roads and a growing outer network

Bengaluru's Outer Ring Road is an arterial city road with junctions and signals, not an expressway, and the tech parks along it make it one of the busiest roads in the country. The tolled NICE Road links parts of the south and west, the Satellite Town Ring Road far outside the city has opened in sections, and a Peripheral Ring Road has been planned for years but was still at an early stage in 2026.

If you are moving from Bengaluru, do not confuse the two ORRs. In Hyderabad, "take the ORR" means an expressway with ramps, tolls and lane speed limits, not a signalled city road.

Which city has the better metro?

Bengaluru's metro is now larger. Its Namma Metro passed 100 km in November 2026, when the Pink Line opened, making it the third Indian city after Delhi-NCR and Mumbai to do so. Hyderabad Metro has three lines, Red, Blue and Green, covering about 69 km, all elevated above the main roads; the Telangana government took full ownership of it from L&T in 2026, and a second phase is planned.

For drivers, the practical point is the same in both cities: a metro line nearby makes it realistic to drive part of the way and ride the rest, especially into areas where parking is hard. Our Hyderabad metro guide for drivers shows where the lines run.

When are the peak hours in each city?

Peak hours follow office times in both cities, and the evening peak is usually the worse of the two. In Hyderabad, plan for roughly 8 to 11 am and 5 to 9 pm on weekdays, with the IT corridor busy late into the evening; TomTom measured an average of about 39 minutes for 10 km in Hyderabad's evening rush in 2025. Bengaluru's peaks are similar in timing, and its higher congestion score suggests they are slower on its worst corridors.

Rain makes both cities much worse, as low junctions and underpasses flood. In Hyderabad, Cyberabad traffic police have repeatedly asked IT companies to stagger log-out times on heavy rain days. See our guides to Hyderabad traffic hotspots and peak hours and the best time to drive in Hyderabad.

Is the driving culture different?

Less than people expect. Both cities have dense, mixed traffic, two-wheelers passing on both sides, autos and buses stopping without warning, and loose lane discipline at busy junctions. A few differences stand out to people who move:

  • U-turns. Hyderabad uses median U-turn openings in place of right turns very widely, so you will often go past your turning and come back.
  • One-ways. Central Bengaluru has an extensive network of one-way streets; central Hyderabad has some, especially in the Old City, but fewer.
  • Speed on open roads. Hyderabad's wider western roads and the ORR allow higher speeds, which makes lane discipline and speed limits matter more.
  • Enforcement. Both cities rely heavily on cameras and e-challans. In Hyderabad, check fines on the Telangana e-challan portal and follow advisories from Hyderabad Traffic Police.

How do parking and two-wheelers compare?

Both cities are dominated by two-wheelers and short of parking in their busiest districts.

  • Two-wheelers. In Bengaluru, two-wheelers made up roughly 70% of more than 1.2 crore registered vehicles in March 2026. Hyderabad's fleet is smaller, at nearly 89 lakh vehicles within Greater Hyderabad in early 2026, and is likewise mostly two-wheelers.
  • Parking. Parking is hard in both cities' old centres and IT districts. In Hyderabad, a state rule limits mall parking fees, with the first 30 minutes free; see our Hyderabad parking guide.

Moving with your car

Your driving licence is valid anywhere in India, though you can update the address on it. A car registered in Karnataka needs re-registration in Telangana if you keep it there for more than 12 months, unless it carries a BH series plate. Getting to know Hyderabad roads in Sawari

If you are moving from Bengaluru, Sawari can help you learn Hyderabad's road layout before you arrive. It is a free browser driving simulator on five real Hyderabad routes, including Cyber Towers to Jubilee Hills over the Durgam Cheruvu cable bridge, Tank Bund, Necklace Road, the Charminar area and Banjara Hills Road No. 1. You meet medians with U-turn openings, flyovers and metro viaducts, and the debrief lists every fault. It runs on phones held sideways with an on-screen steering wheel, tablets, laptops and PCs. Try the HITEC City route first.

Sawari is for practice only. It does not replace lessons with a licensed instructor or real driving experience. On the road, follow the signs, signals and instructions from the traffic police.
