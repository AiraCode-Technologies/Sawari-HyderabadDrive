Driving in Hyderabad for beginners is less frightening if you build up in stages: quiet roads at quiet times first, then busier junctions and flyovers, and the narrow, crowded Old City last. This guide covers what to expect from the city's traffic and a simple order for gaining confidence.

It assumes you are learning with an instructor or a licensed driver beside you, as the law requires on a learner's licence. If you have not started the paperwork yet, see our guide to getting a driving licence in Hyderabad.

Your first drives

The first few drives are about getting to know the car, not the city. Before you go anywhere busy:

  • Set up properly. Seat close enough that your wrists rest on top of the wheel with your arms straight; mirrors adjusted so you can see along both sides of the car; seatbelt on before the engine starts.
  • Learn the car's size. In an empty space, practise stopping close to a line or a marker, and notice where it sits in your view. This helps enormously later in traffic.
  • Practise smooth control. Moving off, stopping and slow turns, until you are not thinking about your feet.
  • Keep sessions short. Concentration fades after about an hour for most new drivers. Stop before you are tired.

India drives on the left. You sit on the right of the car, with the centre line close to your right shoulder. Many beginners drift left towards the kerb because they are not used to judging the left side of the car. Focus on the middle of your lane.

Calmer times and areas

Hyderabad's traffic changes a lot through the day. Picking your time is the easiest way to make a drive easier.

Times

  • Early morning is usually calmest, particularly at weekends.
  • Weekday rush hours, morning and evening, are hard work, especially on routes to and from the IT areas.
  • Evenings around Hussain Sagar get busy with visitors and people walking by the lake.
  • Night brings fewer vehicles but more glare and some faster drivers. Leave it until your daytime driving is steady; our night driving tips cover what changes.
  • Rain slows everything and hides potholes under water. Avoid your first drives in a heavy shower.

Areas

Wide roads with few turns and a median are the gentlest start: you have a clear lane and no oncoming traffic to worry about. The lakeside roads around Hussain Sagar are good examples. Commercial areas with shops along the road, crowded markets and narrow residential lanes come later. The Old City around the Charminar, with its tight streets, stalls and dense crowds, is the hardest of all.

Understanding Hyderabad traffic

Two-wheelers

Scooters and motorbikes are everywhere, and they will pass you on both sides, sometimes in gaps you did not know existed. You cannot control that, but you can be predictable: indicate early, change direction gradually, check your mirrors before every move, and look over your shoulder before turning left, where a rider may be coming up beside you. When you stop, leave a little space so you are not boxed in.

Autos and buses

Autorickshaws stop to pick up and drop passengers wherever they are hailed, often with little warning. Buses pull in and out of stops and need space. Hang back from both, and do not try to squeeze past a bus that is pulling out. Our guide to sharing the road with autos, buses and two-wheelers goes further.

Horns

You will hear a lot of horns. In Hyderabad traffic a short toot usually means "I am here", not anger. Do not let it rush you into a move you are not ready for. Use your own horn sparingly: a short tap to warn someone who may not have seen you, not to hurry people. Horns are prohibited in some zones, such as near hospitals, where signs say so.

Pedestrians

People cross where they need to, not only at zebra crossings, and walk on the road where the footpath is blocked. Slow down near markets, bus stops, schools and places of worship.

Signals, junctions and free left turns

At a signal, stop behind the stop line on red. Amber means stop unless you are too close to stop safely; it is not a cue to speed up. When the light turns green, look before you go: someone may still be crossing. Where traffic police are directing a junction, their signals override the lights.

Some Hyderabad junctions have a free left: you may turn left without waiting for green. Only do this where it is clearly allowed, for example by a sign, a green left arrow or a separate slip lane, and still give way to pedestrians and to traffic you are joining. Where there is no such indication, wait for the signal.

If you are going straight or turning right, do not stop in the left-most lane at a free left junction. Blocking it holds up everyone behind you, and it is a common complaint from Hyderabad drivers and the traffic police alike.

Indicate before every turn and every change of lane, in good time. Get into the correct lane early; painted arrows show which lane goes where. If you miss a turn, carry on and find the next legal U-turn rather than cutting across. See our Hyderabad traffic rules guide for more, and the Hyderabad Traffic Police website for official notices.

Parking basics

  • Use proper parking: marked bays, pay parking and the parking areas at malls and offices.
  • Do not park on footpaths, at junctions or bends, on zebra crossings, at bus stops, in front of gates, or where signs say no parking.
  • Leave room for others to get out, and for the car in front and behind to move.
  • Vehicles parked in the wrong place may be towed or fined. Check the signs every time, even somewhere you have parked before.
  • Practise parking in a quiet car park before you try it on a busy street with people waiting.
Before you get out, check your mirror and look back for two-wheelers. Opening a door into a passing scooter is an easy mistake and a dangerous one.

A progression plan: easy roads to the Old City

Build up one step at a time. Move on only when the current stage feels routine, not just survivable.

  1. An empty space. Controls, starting, stopping, slow turns.
  2. Quiet residential roads at a quiet time. Keeping left, indicating, simple junctions.
  3. Tank Bund Road and Necklace Road. Wide, steady lakeside roads with few turns, good for lane discipline, speed control and getting used to two-wheelers around you. Go early in the day. Our Tank Bund Road guide describes what to expect.
  4. HITEC City and Jubilee Hills. Multi-lane roads, flyovers, signals and merging traffic. Plan your lane before each junction, and start outside rush hour.
  5. The Old City. Narrow streets, crowds, stalls near the kerb, parked two-wheelers and roundabouts. Go slowly, with an experienced driver beside you, and accept that progress will be slow.

Rehearsing the same order in a simulator

Tank Bund Road and Necklace Road are the easy routes, Cyber Towers to Jubilee Hills and Banjara Hills Road No. 1 are medium, and the Charminar and Pathergatti route is hard. It is a free browser simulator on real Hyderabad roads with left-hand traffic, local traffic and a debrief that lists every fault, from missed indicators to red lights.

But driving a route on screen before you drive it for real means the layout, the turns and the busy spots are no surprise. Start with Tank Bund Road and light traffic, and add more as you go.

On a learner's licence, always drive with a licensed driver beside you and L plates on the car. Do not drive tired, and never use your phone while driving.
