After a car accident in Hyderabad, first make the scene safe and switch on your hazard lights, then call 112 if anyone is hurt or the road is blocked. Help the injured without moving them unless they are in danger, report the accident to the police, take photos, swap details and tell your insurer as soon as you can.

Accidents are frightening, and it is easy to forget what to do. This guide gives the steps in order, along with what the law says about helping victims, as of November 2026. It is general information, not legal advice; if your accident is serious, speak to the police and, if needed, a lawyer.

What should you do in the first few minutes?

Stop, make yourself and the scene safe, and check whether anyone is hurt; everything else can wait.

  1. Stop the car. Do not drive away from an accident you are involved in, even a minor one.
  2. Switch on the hazard lights and, if you have one, place a warning triangle well behind the car.
  3. Switch off the engine and apply the handbrake. If there is a smell of fuel, keep everyone away from the car and do not smoke.
  4. Get out on the safe side, away from moving traffic, and stand behind a barrier or on the footpath. On a flyover or the Outer Ring Road, traffic is fast, so do not stand between vehicles.
  5. Check for injuries, in your car first and then in the other vehicles. Look for pedestrians and two-wheeler riders who may have been thrown clear.
  6. Move the cars only if nobody is hurt, the damage is minor and the cars are blocking traffic. Take photos first.
Stay calm and polite. Arguments after an accident can escalate quickly, especially if a crowd gathers. If you feel unsafe, stay in your locked car and call 112.

Which emergency number should you call in Telangana?

Call 112. It is Telangana's single emergency number for police, ambulance and fire, and it works from any phone.

Telangana Police moved its emergency response to 112 under the national Emergency Response Support System, and launched an upgraded 112 platform at the Telangana Integrated Command and Control Centre in Hyderabad in August 2026, replacing the old practice of calling Dial 100 for the police. The 108 ambulance service continues to run in Telangana, so you can also call 108 for an ambulance.

When you call, give the exact location: the road name, the nearest landmark, flyover or metro station, and which direction you were travelling. Say how many people are hurt and whether anyone is trapped or unconscious.

How can you help the injured, and are you protected?

Help as far as you safely can and get medical help fast; the law protects people who help accident victims in good faith.

Basic help

  • Do not move an injured person unless they are in immediate danger, such as from fire or traffic. Moving someone with a neck or back injury can make it worse.
  • Talk to them and keep them calm and warm.
  • Press firmly on heavy bleeding with a clean cloth.
  • Do not give food or water to a seriously injured person.
  • Do not remove a rider's helmet unless they cannot breathe.

Good Samaritan protection

Section 134A, added to the Motor Vehicles Act in 2019, says a Good Samaritan, someone who helps in good faith and voluntarily, without expecting a reward, is not liable in any civil or criminal action for injury or death of the victim caused by their negligence in giving emergency help. The Ministry of Road Transport and Highways then notified rules for Good Samaritans on 29 September 2020 (G.S.R. 594(E)). Under them:

  • no police officer or other person can force you to give your name, address or other personal details;
  • if you only informed the police or took the victim to hospital, you cannot be put through further requirements by the police or the hospital;
  • hospitals must display a charter of Good Samaritans' rights, and give you an acknowledgement if you ask for one;
  • being a witness is voluntary.

The government's Rah-Veer scheme also rewards a Good Samaritan who saves the life of a victim of a serious road accident with Rs 25,000. In addition, a central scheme, now called PM RAHAT, provides eligible accident victims with cashless treatment of up to Rs 1.5 lakh for seven days at designated hospitals; calling 112 can link the victim to one.

These protections are for bystanders. If you are the driver involved, the law still requires you to stop, try to get medical help for anyone injured and report the accident.

When do you need to report the accident to the police?

If anyone is injured or killed, the accident must be reported to the police; for damage only, a report is often still wise.

Under Section 134 of the Motor Vehicles Act, a driver involved in an accident in which someone is injured, or third-party property is damaged, must take reasonable steps to get medical attention for the injured, give the police the information they ask for and report the accident at the nearest police station if no officer is present. Not doing so is an offence.

In practice, police who attend will note the details, and in an injury case a First Information Report (FIR) is usually registered at the police station that covers the area. Ask for the FIR number or a copy, because your insurer and any compensation claim will need it. In Hyderabad, the city is policed by the Hyderabad, Cyberabad and Rachakonda commissionerates; the Hyderabad Traffic Police site has contact details.

For a small scrape with nobody hurt, the drivers often agree to settle it themselves. That is your choice, but it means you cannot easily claim on insurance later if the other driver changes their mind, so think before you skip the police.

What evidence should you collect?

Photos, details and names, collected calmly at the scene, make everything that follows easier.

  • Photos of both cars from several angles, the damage up close, number plates, the road layout, signals, skid marks and any road signs, before the cars are moved if possible.
  • The other driver's details: name, phone number, vehicle number, and their insurer and policy number if they will share it.
  • Witnesses: names and phone numbers of anyone who saw it and is willing to help.
  • Dashcam footage, if you have a dashcam. Save the clip before it is recorded over.
  • Notes: the time, place and what happened, written down while you remember it.

Do not admit fault or sign anything at the scene beyond what the police ask for. Stick to the facts.

How do you make an insurance claim?

Tell your insurer as soon as you can, follow its process, and do not start repairs until it says so.

  1. Call your insurer's claim helpline or use its app, and get a claim number.
  2. Keep the documents it asks for ready. These usually include your policy, registration certificate, driving licence and, for injury or theft cases, the FIR.
  3. Let the insurer's surveyor inspect the damage, usually at a network garage for cashless repair.
  4. If another vehicle was involved and someone was injured, a third-party claim goes through the Motor Accidents Claims Tribunal, so inform your insurer straight away.

Can a simulator help you avoid accidents?

It can help you build the habits that prevent many of them. Sawari is a free browser driving simulator on five real Hyderabad routes, and its debrief lists every fault, including collisions, red lights, missed indicators and speed breakers taken too fast. It runs on a phone with an on-screen steering wheel, a tablet, a laptop or a PC. It does not simulate crashes or what to do after them, and it is practice only.
