Version 1.5.0 is about the HITEC City drive and the structures along it: the Durgam Cheruvu cable bridge now looks and drives like the real one, flyovers are lit, and the metro stands on proper piers. Slopes on every route are smoother too.

The Durgam Cheruvu cable bridge

  • The two pylons now stand at either side of the lake, about 234 m apart, as on the real bridge (its main span is 233.85 m). Before, they stood close together mid-lake and were easy to miss.
  • Taller white portal pylons, and stay cables you can actually see, fanned across the main span and the back spans.
  • No piers in the lake under the main span: the real bridge crosses the water between its pylons with nothing below.

Getting on and off bridges

  • You can drive off the far end of the Durgam Cheruvu bridge onto Road No. 45. The car was being held back where the three-lane bridge met the two-lane road.
  • Where a narrower road joins a bridge or flyover, it now tapers from the deck's width to its own over 40 m, like a real merge, instead of stepping in.
  • The barrier that keeps you on a deck only pushes you back from the edge. It never stops you driving on into a slip road or the road beyond.

Street lights on flyovers and bridges

Flyovers and bridges had no lights. They now have lamps on their parapets about every 30 m: along the outer edge of one-way decks and on both edges of two-way ones. There are about 300 on the HITEC City route alone, including the whole cable bridge.

Metro piers

  • One round pier at a time, in the middle of the median under the line, about every 30 m, as on the Hyderabad Metro. Piers no longer step sideways or stand in pairs.
  • Metro stations rest on the same single round piers, without extra square columns.
  • Medians under the metro are wide enough for the piers.

Smoother slopes

Roads now follow the ground in steps of about 4 m instead of 10 m, with heights to the centimetre, so climbs like Banjara Hills Road No. 1 rise evenly instead of in visible steps.
