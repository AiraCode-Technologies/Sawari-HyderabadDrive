Hyderabad has built dozens of flyovers and underpasses in the last decade, and this list covers the ones that matter most for daily drives: what each one bypasses, where it lands and what to watch for. For how to drive any flyover well, from lane choice to merging, see our separate guide to driving on flyovers in Hyderabad; this post is about the roads themselves.

Hyderabad flyovers and the SRDP

Most of the city's newer flyovers come from the Strategic Road Development Plan (SRDP), a programme run through the Greater Hyderabad Municipal Corporation (GHMC) to take through traffic over the worst junctions. It covers flyovers, underpasses, road-over-bridges and steel bridges, and many of them are concentrated in the IT corridor around HITEC City, Gachibowli and Kondapur, and on the eastern side around LB Nagar.

The idea is the same everywhere: through traffic goes over or under the junction, while traffic that wants to turn uses the service road and the signal. That only works if you choose the right lane before the ramp, which is why knowing the city's main flyovers in advance is so useful.

The big elevated roads

PV Narasimha Rao Expressway

Opened in 2009 and about 11.6 km long, this is still Hyderabad's longest flyover. It runs on pillars from Mehdipatnam towards Aramghar, above the road below, and is the main route from the western and central city to Rajiv Gandhi International Airport. Exits are few, so plan which ramp you need before you join. Our flyover driving guide covers the expressway's long ramps and merges.

Aramghar to Zoo Park flyover

Opened in January 2025, this six-lane flyover is a little over 4 km long, making it the city's second longest. Built under SRDP, it lifts traffic over the junctions between Aramghar and the Nehru Zoological Park, including Shastripuram, on the busy southern approach to the city. It has been named after former Prime Minister Manmohan Singh. It is worth knowing if you drive between the airport side and the Old City or Bahadurpura.

The IT corridor: HITEC City, Gachibowli and Jubilee Hills

Shaikpet flyover

Opened on 1 January 2022, the Shaikpet flyover is a six-lane road about 2.7 km long, from Tolichowki towards Raidurg. It passes over several busy junctions, including Seven Tombs, Film Nagar Road and OU Colony, on one of the main links between the older western city and Gachibowli. It is long and busy at peak hours; get into your lane at Tolichowki and stay in it.

Durgam Cheruvu cable bridge

The cable-stayed bridge across Durgam Cheruvu opened in September 2020. It links Jubilee Hills Road No. 45, via an elevated approach, with Madhapur and the HITEC City side of the lake. It has become a landmark, lit up at night, and that brings its own hazard: people stopping to take photos. Never stop on the carriageway; enjoy the view from the footpaths and viewpoints.

Biodiversity Junction and Kothaguda

Two multi-level flyovers sort out some of the most complicated junctions in the IT corridor. The Biodiversity Junction flyovers, near Gachibowli, opened in stages in 2019 and 2020. The Kothaguda multi-level flyover opened on 1 January 2023, with a main flyover of about 2.2 km plus ramps, taking traffic over the Botanical Garden and Kothaguda junctions towards Kondapur and HITEC City.

Multi-level flyovers are the ones where wrong ramp choices happen most. The signs come thick and fast, and the ramps split close together. If you do this route daily, learn the layout once on a quiet day.

Underpasses

The IT corridor also has several underpasses, such as the ones at Ayyappa Society and Mindspace junctions. Underpasses have their own risks: water collects at the lowest point in heavy rain, and the light changes sharply as you go in and out. Central and eastern Hyderabad

Indira Park to VST steel bridge

Opened in August 2023, this four-lane, two-way steel flyover runs about 2.6 km from near Lower Tank Bund and Indira Park to VST, over the busy roads around Ashok Nagar and RTC X Roads. It is named after Nayani Narasimha Reddy. It saves several signals between the Tank Bund area and VST, and the newer layout catches out drivers who still expect the old junctions.

Punjagutta steel bridges

Short steel bridges at Punjagutta, opened in 2020 and 2022, ease movements at one of the city's busiest junctions. They are short, but the ramps start and end close to other turns, so look well ahead.

LB Nagar and the Vijayawada road

The LB Nagar area has a cluster of SRDP flyovers and underpasses, including flyovers at LB Nagar, Kamineni and Bairamalguda, many opened around 2020. Together they let through traffic on the Vijayawada highway skip a series of signals near the end of the metro's Red Line. Lanes split often here; read the overhead signs early.

Tips for daily flyover routes

  • Learn your landing point. For each flyover on your commute, know where it comes down and what is just after the landing: a junction, a bus stop, a speed breaker or a U-turn. Most surprises happen there.
  • Know your exit ramps. On long elevated roads such as the PV Narasimha Rao Expressway, there are only a few ways off. Count them on your first few drives so you are never caught out.
  • Use the road below when you need to turn. A flyover saves time only if you are going straight on. If your turn is at the junction it crosses, stay on the service road.
  • Check the map before new routes. Recently opened flyovers may not yet appear in older maps or navigation data, and navigation apps sometimes confuse a flyover with the road beneath it. If the voice says "turn left" while you are on a flyover, carry on to the landing.
  • Leave more time in the monsoon. The roads at the foot of ramps and in underpasses are the first to flood.

Still being built

More projects are under way. A steel bridge from Malakpet towards Santosh Nagar, about 2.5 km long, was reported in February 2026 as under construction, with an expected opening around the end of 2026. Other flyovers and elevated corridors have been proposed or started across the city. Opening dates slip often, and roads near works have narrowed lanes, diversions and night closures, so allow extra time and follow the signs.

Flyovers in Hyderabad are sometimes closed at night, on special occasions such as New Year's Eve, or to certain vehicles. Closures are announced by the traffic police. Check the Hyderabad Traffic Police for current advisories before late-night drives.

Flyovers in Sawari

Sawari is a free browser driving simulator on five real Hyderabad routes, built from OpenStreetMap with real elevation, so its flyovers rise and fall as they do in life. Of the roads above, only the Durgam Cheruvu cable bridge and its approaches are in the game, on the Cyber Towers to Jubilee Hills route, which also crosses flyover ramps and merges near Inorbit Mall. It is a good place to practise picking a lane before a ramp, with the blue lane band showing which way to go. Read our route guide and start the drive. Sawari is for practice and does not replace real lessons.
