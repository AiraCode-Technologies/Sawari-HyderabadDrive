The RTA driving test in Hyderabad is short, and most people who fail it do so for the same handful of reasons: rolling back, touching a marker, forgetting to indicate or check mirrors, or simply letting nerves take over. These Telangana RTA driving test tips cover what examiners look for and how to practise so the test feels routine.

A note before we start: test formats vary between offices and change over time. Some RTA offices use dedicated test tracks with marked lanes; the details of each track, and how it is scored, are best learned from your driving school or the office itself. This post sticks to what is true of any fair driving test, and it is not an official guide. For the current rules, check the Telangana Transport Department website.

What examiners look for

An examiner is not looking for a racing driver. They want evidence that you can handle the car on your own without putting anyone at risk. In practice that comes down to five things.

Control

Smooth starts, smooth stops, and steady low-speed steering. In a manual car this means clutch control: moving off without stalling or jerking, and holding the car on a slope without rolling back. Jerky, hurried movements suggest you are not in control even if you finish the course.

Observation

Checking your mirrors before you move off, before you change direction and before you slow down. Looking over your shoulder before pulling out. Examiners notice whether your head and eyes move, not just whether you avoided something.

Signalling

Indicating in good time before every turn or change of position, and cancelling the indicator afterwards. A signal that comes after you have started turning tells the examiner you forgot.

Following the rules

Keeping left, obeying signs and markings, stopping fully where you must, and keeping to a sensible speed. If you are tested on a road or a course with signals, a stop line means the front of your car stops behind it.

Composure

Mistakes happen. What matters is what you do next. If you stall, put the handbrake on, restart calmly and carry on. Panicking after a small mistake often causes a bigger one.

Common reasons people fail

Driving schools and instructors in Hyderabad tend to name the same faults again and again. None of them are about knowledge; they are about habits.

  • Rolling back or stalling on a slope. If your office tests a start on an incline, this is the classic fail. Practise the handbrake start until it is boring.
  • Touching a line, kerb or marker. On a marked course, going outside the lines or knocking a pole usually counts heavily. This is a matter of slow speed and reference points, not luck.
  • No mirror checks. Many learners do look, but only with their eyes and too quickly to be noticed. Make the check deliberate.
  • Late or missing indicators. Indicate before you brake and turn, not during.
  • Going too fast in tight spaces. Speed makes every manoeuvre harder. Creep. There is no prize for finishing quickly.
  • Not following the examiner's instruction. Listen to the whole instruction before you move. If you did not hear it, ask them to repeat it.
  • Paperwork problems. An expired learner's licence, a car without valid documents or missing L plates can stop the test before it starts.
Ask your instructor to give you a mock test on the same kind of course, marked as strictly as the real one. Hearing "that would have been a fail" in a lesson is much cheaper than hearing it on the day.

A four-week practice plan

You must hold your learner's licence for at least 30 days before the test, which fits neatly with a four-week plan. Adjust it around your lessons; the point is steady practice rather than cramming. (If you have not got your learner's licence yet, start with our guide to getting a driving licence in Hyderabad.)

Week 1: the car

Get to know the controls with your instructor in a quiet place. Seat and mirror adjustment, moving off and stopping, clutch control at walking pace, steering hand positions. Do not hurry onto busy roads.

Week 2: habits

Build the routine you will use on test day: mirrors, signal, manoeuvre. Every turn, every time. Practise stopping smoothly at a line. On quieter roads, work on keeping a steady position on the left of your lane. Our post on indicators and lane discipline goes into this in more detail.

Week 3: the manoeuvres

Find out what your office tests and practise exactly that. If it includes reversing between markers, parking in a marked bay or a start on a slope, set up something similar with your instructor and repeat it many times. Pick reference points on the car, such as where a line meets the edge of the bonnet or the mirror, so you are not guessing.

Week 4: test conditions

Do full mock tests. Drive the course start to finish without coaching, then talk through what went wrong. Practise at the time of day your slot is booked if you can. In the last few days, do less, not more: you want to arrive calm and rested.

Where a simulator fits in

A driving simulator cannot teach clutch control, the bite point, or how hard to press a real brake pedal. It cannot test you on an RTA track. What it can do is drill the decisions that become habits, at home, as often as you like.

Sawari is a free browser simulator on real Hyderabad roads. It drives on the left with a right-hand-drive car and an automatic gearbox, and it logs faults as they happen: no indicator before a turn, running a red light, speeding, drifting right of the centre line, leaving the road, collisions and taking speed breakers too fast. The debrief at the end lists each one with advice. Repeating a route until the debrief is clean is a good way to make "signal before every turn" automatic.

On the day of the test

  • Check your documents the night before: learner's licence, application acknowledgement, originals of what you uploaded, and the car's papers if you are bringing it.
  • Arrive early. Rushing in late is a poor start to a test of calm driving.
  • Adjust your seat and mirrors before you start, even in a familiar car. Examiners often watch this.
  • Put your seatbelt on before you start the engine.
  • Drive the way you have practised, a little slower if anything.
  • If you make a mistake, keep going. Small errors are not always a fail; giving up is.
Never drive alone on a learner's licence to "get extra practice" before the test. Always have a person holding a valid licence beside you and L plates on the car.

If you do not pass

It is common to fail a driving test the first time, and it is not a judgement on whether you will be a good driver. Ask what went wrong, write it down, and practise that specific thing. The portal explains when you can book again. Check that your learner's licence will still be valid on the new date, because it lasts six months from issue.

Once you have your licence, the real learning starts on real roads. When you want to rehearse a busy route before you drive it, you can open Sawari in your browser and try it first.
