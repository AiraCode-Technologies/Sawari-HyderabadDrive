Version 1.8.0 is about how a drive sounds and feels: the car now sounds like a real small petrol car, the spoken directions talk like a navigation app, and every slope in the game rises smoothly.

An engine that sounds like an engine

  • The engine is now built from the firing of its cylinders, the way a real one makes its sound, instead of steady tones. A four-cylinder fires twice every turn of the crank: about 28 times a second at idle and 200 near the redline.
  • The four cylinders are never quite equal, so the idle has the gentle, lumpy beat of a real engine and a low rumble underneath.
  • It is soft and muffled when you cruise, and opens up into a harder, raspier note when you put your foot down and the revs climb.
  • Browsers that cannot run it keep the previous engine sound.

Directions like a navigation app

  • The most natural voice on your device is used, Indian English first: Microsoft's natural voices on Edge, Google's voices on Chrome and Android, and Apple's on iPhone, iPad and Mac.
  • Turns are announced about 500 metres out at speed, again at about 200 metres, and a few seconds before the turn itself, earlier the faster you are going.
  • Distances are rounded the way navigation apps round them, and two turns close together are joined: "Turn left onto Jubilee Hills Road No. 36, then turn right."
  • After a turn with a long road ahead you hear how far to keep going: "Continue on HITEC City Road for 1.2 kilometres."
  • The drive starts with the direction to head in, and the engine and road noise dip while the voice speaks.

Smooth slopes everywhere

  • The ground is now shaped to the centimetre. Gentle hills used to step up in flat terraces 10 cm high; they now rise smoothly.
  • Every road on the ground has a smooth profile: it still follows the hills in 4 metre steps, but the slope now changes only a tiny amount from one step to the next, so climbs like Banjara Hills Road No. 1 are even all the way up.
  • Where two junctions sit close together, the road between them no longer jumps up or down: their heights are eased so that short link stays gentle.
  • Flyovers and bridges keep their own ramps. Routes, turns and lengths are unchanged.

Try it

Drive up Banjara Hills Road No. 1 to feel the smoother climb, or take the HITEC City route to hear the new directions over ten turns and the cable bridge.
