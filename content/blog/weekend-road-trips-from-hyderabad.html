The best weekend road trips from Hyderabad are all within a few hours' drive: forest hills at Ananthagiri, temples at Yadagirigutta and Srisailam, weaving villages at Pochampally, and history at Warangal and Bidar. Getting there means leaving city traffic for highways, where the speeds, the trucks and the two-lane overtaking need a different set of habits.

This guide gives rough distances and the roads you are likely to use, then the highway skills that make the drive safe. Distances are approximate and depend on where in the city you start, so check a map app for your own trip.

Short drives: Pochampally and Yadagirigutta

Bhoodan Pochampally, known for its ikat handloom, and the hill temple at Yadagirigutta are both east of the city and close enough for an easy half-day drive. They are good first trips if you have not driven out of Hyderabad before: you get a taste of highway speeds without hours of it. Yadagirigutta is very busy at weekends and festivals, so expect queues for parking near the temple.

Ananthagiri Hills and Vikarabad

Ananthagiri, a few kilometres from Vikarabad town, is the nearest green hill escape, with forest, viewpoints and the Anantha Padmanabha Swamy temple. The usual way is west through Moinabad and Chevella on the Hyderabad to Bijapur road. Be careful on this stretch. It has long been a narrow road with heavy truck traffic and a poor accident record, and widening work has been under way. Drive it slowly, expect diversions, and do not try to overtake in a hurry.

The last part climbs through the hills on a winding road. Take it in a low gear, keep left on the bends and watch for two-wheelers and people walking near the viewpoints.

Warangal and Bidar

Warangal, with its fort and the Thousand Pillar Temple, is a straightforward drive on the Warangal highway, a busy national highway with plenty of trucks and buses. Bidar, just across the border in Karnataka, has a large fort and Bahmani-era monuments, reached by heading west on the Mumbai highway. Both work as long day trips, though an overnight stay lets you see them without rushing the drive home.

Nagarjuna Sagar

The dam and reservoir at Nagarjuna Sagar are reached by leaving the city on the Outer Ring Road and taking the Nagarjuna Sagar highway through Ibrahimpatnam. Parts of the route are two-lane with villages along the way, so the drive takes longer than the distance suggests. If you are going in the monsoon to see the gates open, expect crowds and plan to park and walk.

Srisailam and the tiger reserve

The drive to Srisailam on NH 765 is the most memorable on this list, and the one that needs the most planning. After Mannanur, the road runs for roughly 55 km through the Amrabad Tiger Reserve to the Domalapenta checkpost, with a long ghat section down towards the Krishna. Inside the reserve, vehicles are not allowed to stop on the forest road, and drivers are asked to go slowly and watch for animals crossing.

Night travel through the reserve is normally not allowed. Forest officials hold vehicles at the checkposts at night and let them continue in the morning. The rule has been relaxed for festivals such as Maha Shivaratri, and the ghat road has been closed at times after heavy rain because of landslides. Timings can change, so check with the forest department or local police before you leave, and plan to clear the forest section well before dark.

Do not stop to photograph animals, feed monkeys or step off the road inside the tiger reserve. It is unsafe for you and for the wildlife, and it is against the rules.

Leaving and coming back to Hyderabad

The city end is often the slowest part of any road trip. Leave early on a Saturday or Sunday morning and you can be out on the highway before traffic builds. Coming back on Sunday evening is the opposite: every highway into the city fills up at the same time, and the last 20 km can take as long as the previous 100.

The Outer Ring Road helps a lot. You can use it to reach the right highway without crossing the city. Our Outer Ring Road guide covers joining it, lane use and exits.

Highway driving tips for the trip

Overtaking on two-lane roads

Many roads beyond the city are a single carriageway with one lane each way. Overtaking there is the most dangerous thing you will do on the trip.

  • Overtake only where you can see a long, clear stretch ahead. Never on a bend, over a crest, near a junction or a village, or where there is a solid centre line.
  • Hang back from the vehicle in front first, so you can see past it. Sitting right on a truck's bumper hides the oncoming traffic from you.
  • Signal, change down a gear if you need the power, pass decisively and come back in only when you can see the overtaken vehicle in your mirror.
  • If you have any doubt, do not go. Waiting a few minutes behind a slow truck costs almost nothing.

Trucks and buses

Heavy vehicles are everywhere on these routes, day and night. Do not linger beside them, give them extra room on bends and climbs, and remember they cannot stop quickly. A truck flashing its headlights at you may be warning you, not inviting you to pass.

Speed, villages and speed breakers

Highways pass straight through villages and towns, often with unmarked speed breakers, cattle, tractors and children near the road. Slow down well before each settlement. Our post on speed breakers and speed limits covers how to take humps safely.

Night driving

Plan to arrive before dark where you can. Unlit highways bring glare from oncoming high beams, vehicles without working tail lights, and people and animals on the road. If you must drive at night, slow down and use dipped beams when anything is coming. See our night driving tips for more.

Tolls and FASTag

National highway toll plazas run on FASTag. At the time of writing (November 2026), vehicles without a valid FASTag pay double the toll in cash or 1.25 times the toll by UPI, under rules that took effect in November 2025. Check your FASTag balance before you leave. If you drive the highways often, the NHAI's FASTag annual pass for private cars may be worth a look; check the current terms on the NHAI website.

Ghat roads and rain

On hill roads, use a lower gear going down rather than riding the brakes, keep left on blind bends and sound the horn briefly where you cannot see round the corner. In the monsoon, ghat roads can have landslides and slippery patches, so check conditions and read our monsoon driving guide before you go.

Before you set off

  • Check tyres, including the spare, and the jack and wheel brace.
  • Carry water, a phone charger and your licence, registration, insurance and pollution certificate (DigiLocker or mParivahan copies are fine).
  • Download offline maps; signal can drop on the forest and hill sections.
  • Take a break every couple of hours, and swap drivers if you can.

Where Sawari fits in

Sawari is a free browser driving simulator on five real Hyderabad routes. It has no highways, no ghat roads and no rain, so it cannot rehearse the trip itself. What it can do is build the city confidence you need for the first and last hour of every road trip: steady lane discipline, signalling, judging gaps and staying calm with buses and two-wheelers around you.

Try the longer Cyber Towers to Jubilee Hills route with traffic set to Dense, and practise looking around with the mouse before each lane change, since the car has no mirrors. Then do your first real highway drive with an experienced driver beside you, on a short trip such as Pochampally or Yadagirigutta, before you take on Srisailam.

Sawari is for practice only. It does not replace lessons with a licensed instructor or real experience on the highway with someone experienced beside you.
