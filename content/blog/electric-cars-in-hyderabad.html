Electric cars in Hyderabad make a strong case for city driving: short daily distances, slow traffic where an EV wastes little energy, and, at the time of writing, no road tax or registration fee in Telangana. The catch is planning: knowing when the tax waiver ends, where you will charge, and how heat and air conditioning affect your range.

This guide covers the rules and money first, then charging at home and in public, then what an EV is actually like to drive on Hyderabad's roads.

EV road tax in Telangana: the waiver and its end date

Telangana brought out its Electric Vehicle and Energy Storage Policy in 2020. In November 2024 the state government issued a government order (widely reported as GO 41) giving a 100 per cent exemption from road tax and registration fee for electric vehicles bought and registered in Telangana. It covers private electric cars as well as two-wheelers, autos, taxis, goods vehicles and buses.

The exemption is valid until 31 December 2026. At the time of writing (November 2026), no extension had been notified. That matters a great deal if you are buying in the next few weeks: road tax on a petrol or diesel car in Telangana is a significant share of the on-road price, and the saving only applies if the car is registered while the order is in force.

If you are buying close to the deadline, ask the dealer exactly when the car will be registered, not just invoiced, and confirm the current position with the Telangana Transport Department. Policies like this can be extended, changed or allowed to lapse.

Green number plates

Battery electric vehicles in India carry green registration plates. Under a 2018 notification from the Ministry of Road Transport and Highways, a private EV has white characters on green and a commercial EV has yellow characters on green. Hybrids are not battery electric vehicles and keep ordinary plates. Our guide to Telangana TG number plates explains the rest of the plate.

Public charging for electric cars in Hyderabad

Public chargers come in two broad kinds, and it helps to know which your car accepts.

  • AC chargers. These feed the car's own on-board charger, so the speed is limited by the car, typically a few kilowatts up to around 11 kW or more on some models. Most new cars sold in India use the Type 2 connector. AC charging suits a stop of a few hours: an office car park, a mall or a hotel.
  • DC fast chargers. These charge the battery directly and are much quicker. Most current electric cars in India use the CCS2 connector. Expect to add a large share of your range in well under an hour, though charging slows as the battery fills, so stopping at around 80 per cent is usually the quickest way to get going again.

Where you tend to find them

Chargers in Hyderabad cluster where people already park: malls, hotels, office campuses in HITEC City and the Financial District, some petrol stations, car dealerships and along the Outer Ring Road and highways out of the city. TGREDCO, the state's renewable energy agency, is the nodal agency for public charging and has been tendering new stations with private operators; a recent tender was reported to plan 200 stations in Hyderabad with a uniform tariff of Rs 13 per kWh. Coverage inside the older, denser parts of the city is thinner, partly because suitable land with a power connection is hard to find.

Apps and payment

Each charging network usually has its own app for finding chargers, starting a session and paying, and there are aggregator apps that list several networks. Before relying on a charger, check in the app that it is working, that it has your connector, and what it costs per unit or per minute. Keep a second option in mind, because a charger shown as free can be occupied or out of order by the time you arrive.

Home charging, including in apartments

For most owners, home charging does the bulk of the work. A car that covers a typical city day can be topped up overnight on a slow AC charger, which is gentler on the battery and far cheaper than public fast charging.

In an independent house it is fairly simple: an electrician checks your sanctioned load and wiring, and a wall box goes in near where you park. Apartments take more organising.

  • Talk to your residents' association first. You will need permission to run a cable from your meter, or from a new connection, to your parking bay, and they will want to know who pays for the power.
  • Billing. The cleanest arrangement is a separate connection or sub-meter for the charger, so your charging is not paid for out of the common-area bill. Ask your power distribution company (TGSPDCL for most of Hyderabad) what it offers for your building.
  • Safety. Use a proper charger installed by a qualified electrician, with its own circuit and protection. Avoid long extension leads in a basement.
  • New buildings. Telangana has been reported to be moving towards requiring charging points in new apartment buildings. If you are buying a flat, ask the builder what is provided.

Range in Hyderabad traffic and heat

City driving is where an electric car does well. In stop-start traffic, a petrol engine burns fuel while idling and wastes energy every time you brake. An EV uses almost nothing while stationary, apart from the air conditioning, and recovers some energy when it slows down. So a slow crawl from Jubilee Hills to HITEC City at peak hour is less of a hit to range than the same time on an open highway.

The bigger factors in Hyderabad are heat and speed:

  • Air conditioning. In April and May, the AC runs hard all the time. In slow traffic it becomes one of the largest uses of energy. Pre-cool the cabin while the car is still plugged in, and park in shade where you can.
  • Battery temperature. Batteries prefer moderate temperatures. Modern EVs manage this themselves, but in strong heat the car may use energy to cool the battery and may limit fast-charging speed.
  • Highway speeds. On the Outer Ring Road or a highway, steady high speed uses much more energy than city driving. If you are planning a longer trip, check where the fast chargers are before you go.
  • Daily charging habits. Many manufacturers suggest charging to around 80 per cent for daily use and keeping 100 per cent for long trips. Follow your car's manual.

The range figure in the brochure is measured on a standard test cycle. Real-world range in Hyderabad will usually be lower, especially in summer with the AC on, so plan with some margin.

Driving an EV on Hyderabad roads

Regenerative braking and one-pedal driving

When you lift off the accelerator, an EV can use its motor to slow the car and put energy back into the battery. Many cars let you choose how strong this is, and some offer a "one-pedal" mode strong enough to bring the car almost to a stop. In Hyderabad traffic this is genuinely useful: you can ease off for a speed breaker, a slowing auto or a red light without touching the brake.

It takes a few days to get used to. Lifting off quickly in a strong regen mode slows the car harder than you may expect, and the driver behind cannot always tell, because brake lights may not come on unless the deceleration is strong enough. Lift off smoothly, keep a good gap, and still use the brake pedal for a proper stop. On a steep, wet descent, a gentler regen setting can feel more predictable.

Quiet at low speed

An electric car is almost silent at walking pace. That is pleasant for you, but people in the road may not hear you coming. In the Old City lanes, in apartment car parks and near schools, assume that pedestrians, children and people stepping off the kerb have not noticed you. Newer EVs make an artificial sound at low speed for this reason, but it is quiet. Go slowly, cover the brake and give people time. The advice in our guide to sharing the road with autos, buses and two-wheelers applies with extra force.

Instant pull

EVs deliver full pulling power from a standstill. That makes them quick away from a signal, but it can catch you out in a crowded junction or on wet roads. Squeeze the accelerator, do not stab it, especially during the monsoon.

Waterlogging

Electric cars are sealed and tested against water, but the same rule applies as with any car: do not drive into water when you cannot see how deep it is. Underpasses in heavy rain are the usual trap.

Practising city skills in Sawari

Sawari's player car behaves like a conventional automatic, not an EV, so it will not teach you one-pedal driving. What it can help with is the part that does not change with the powertrain: reading traffic, keeping a gap, signalling and taking speed breakers smoothly on real Hyderabad roads rebuilt from OpenStreetMap. The HITEC City to Jubilee Hills route is a good one to practise the kind of daily commute where many people in the city drive EVs. It is free in the browser on a laptop or desktop.
