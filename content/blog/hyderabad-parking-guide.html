Parking in Hyderabad is easiest in malls, office campuses and paid parking lots, and hardest in the Old City, on busy commercial roads and around hospitals. The safe rule is simple: park only where it is clearly allowed, because traffic police tow and fine wrongly parked vehicles and many fines arrive as camera e-challans.

This guide covers the places drivers ask about most, as of November 2026. We have left out parking rates, because they change often and vary by operator; check the signboard or app at each place.

Is mall parking free in Hyderabad?

Partly. A Telangana government order that came into force on 1 April 2018 sets limits on what malls and multiplexes in Hyderabad can charge:

  • Up to 30 minutes: no parking fee.
  • Up to one hour: free if you show a purchase bill from the mall.
  • More than one hour: free if your bill or movie ticket is worth more than the parking fee; otherwise the normal fee applies.

Not every mall has followed it. GHMC inspection teams found malls charging customers who had purchase receipts and issued notices in 2024, and GHMC has fined malls for illegal parking fee collection. Keep your bill and parking ticket, and if you are wrongly charged, complain to the mall and to GHMC.

Mall basements are tight. Follow the arrows, drive at walking pace with your headlights on, and watch for pillars and people walking between cars. On weekend evenings the queue to get in can back up onto the main road, so arriving early helps.

Where can you park near Charminar and in the Old City?

Hardly anywhere close by, so plan to park at a distance. The streets around the Charminar, Gulzar Houz, Laad Bazaar and Pathergatti are narrow and crowded, and shoppers and traders have long complained about the lack of parking.

  • The planned car park is not built. GHMC has planned a multi-level parking complex on the Charminar bus stand and Khilwat Khazana building sites, but as of November 2026 the project was stalled after its tenders drew no response.
  • Festival parking. During Ramzan and other busy seasons, traffic police have opened temporary parking at places such as the Charminar bus station ground, Khilwat and the Quli Qutub Shah Stadium. Look for the announcements from Hyderabad Traffic Police before you go.
  • Use an auto for the last stretch. Parking further out and taking an auto or walking is often quicker than circling the lanes.

If you do drive in, go early in the day and read our Charminar and Old City driving guide first.

How does parking work at HITEC City offices and hospitals?

Most large tech parks in HITEC City, Madhapur, Gachibowli and the Financial District have basement or multi-level parking for employees, usually with a pass, a sticker or a tag. Visitor parking is limited, and some campuses ask visitors to register at the gate.

  • Ask your host whether visitor parking is available before you drive to a meeting.
  • Do not park on the service roads or outside the gates unless it is signed as allowed. These roads carry heavy traffic at log-in and log-out times and are watched closely.
  • Consider the metro. The Blue Line serves HITEC City and Raidurg, so driving to a station with parking and riding in can be easier than finding a space in the IT corridor.

Hospitals

Hospital parking fills up quickly, especially in the morning outpatient rush. If you are bringing a patient, drop them at the entrance first and then look for a space. Never block the ambulance bay or the emergency entrance, even for a minute. At large hospitals on busy roads, a cab or auto may be easier for routine appointments.

Can you park at Hyderabad metro stations?

At some of them. Parking is mostly at or near the ends of the lines, where stations have more land; many stations in the city centre have little or none.

  • Paid parking was introduced at Miyapur and Nagole stations in 2024 by the then operator, L&T Metro Rail. The Telangana government took over the Phase I network from L&T in 2026, so check the current arrangements at the station.
  • Nampally has a fully automated multi-level parking complex built by Hyderabad Metro Rail, opened in January 2026. Cars are moved and stored by machine after you leave them at the entry bay.
  • Do not park under the viaduct or on the road outside a station unless signs allow it. The roads below the metro are busy and often narrowed by pillars.

What are the rules for street parking in Hyderabad?

Park only where it is allowed, clear of traffic, and never where you block others. In general, do not park:

  • where a No Parking or No Stopping sign applies;
  • on a footpath, a zebra crossing or near a junction;
  • at or near a bus stop, or in a free left lane;
  • on a flyover, a bridge or the Outer Ring Road, except in an emergency;
  • in front of gates and driveways, fire hydrants or hospital entrances;
  • double parked beside another vehicle.

GHMC runs paid on-street parking at some locations, and its parking policy has generally allowed on-street parking only on wider roads. Where you see attendants, ask for a receipt. Our Hyderabad traffic rules guide covers the wider rules.

No-parking fines and towing

Traffic police tow cars and two-wheelers parked in no-parking zones or blocking traffic, and photograph wrongly parked vehicles for e-challans. If your vehicle has gone, call the local traffic police station or the Hyderabad Traffic Police helpline to find out where it was taken. You will normally pay a towing charge and a fine. Check pending challans on the Telangana e-challan portal.

Before you walk away, look back at your car: is it fully off the carriageway, clear of any gate, and not under a no-parking sign you missed? It takes ten seconds and saves a trip to the tow yard.

Parking tips that save time

  • Decide where you will park before you leave. Map apps show many paid lots, and choosing one in advance stops you circling.
  • Arrive early for weekend malls, festivals, cricket matches and the Numaish exhibition.
  • Reverse into bays where you can. Driving out forwards into a busy lane is safer.
  • Fold your mirrors on narrow streets and leave room for two-wheelers to pass.
  • Keep valuables out of sight and lock the car, even in staffed lots.
  • Leave a phone number on the dashboard in residential areas so a neighbour can call rather than complain.

Practising crowded streets in Sawari

Sawari does not simulate parking manoeuvres, so it will not teach you to reverse into a bay. What it can help with is driving the crowded approach to places where parking is hard. Its Charminar and Pathergatti route has narrow streets and busy kerbs, and the debrief lists every fault so you can see where you came too close or went too fast. It runs free in a browser on phones held sideways with an on-screen steering wheel, tablets, laptops and PCs.

Sawari is for practice only. It does not replace lessons with a licensed instructor. On the road, follow the signs, signals and instructions from the traffic police.
