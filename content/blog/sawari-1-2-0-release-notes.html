Version 1.2.0 goes through the HITEC City route stretch by stretch and rebuilds it to match the real road: its lanes, its median, the metro above it and the Durgam Cheruvu cable bridge.

Full-width roads

The map sometimes draws the two halves of a divided road closer together than their lanes need. Sawari used to squeeze the whole road to fit its tightest spot, which shrank the three-lane bridge corridor to one lane's width. Now the two halves move apart where they crowd, so HITEC City Road keeps its four lanes each way and the bridge corridor its three, with a real median between them.

The metro over the median

Hyderabad's metro runs above the middle of the road. The viaduct now sits over the median of HITEC City Road and Jubilee Hills Road No. 36, the median is wide enough for its pillars, and the stations span the whole road on columns in the median and on the footpaths.

Durgam Cheruvu bridge and Road No. 45

  • The bridge and the elevated corridor keep at least 6.5 metres above the ground beneath, and about 26 metres above the lake, instead of cutting into the hills.
  • The slip roads that join the corridor rise to meet it, with no step where they join.
  • The cable-stayed pylon spans both decks, with stays fanned to each.
  • There are no U-turn openings on the bridge or the flyover.

Smaller fixes

  • Roundabouts have a raised, planted island in the middle, like the one at Mindspace. See how to drive a roundabout.
  • Bus shelters stand on the kerbside footpath, never in a median.
  • No billboards stand against a flyover deck.

Drive it

The best way to see the difference is to drive from Cyber Towers to Jubilee Hills.
