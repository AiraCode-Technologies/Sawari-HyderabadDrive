Hyderabad to Vijayawada by road: the NH 65 drive, tolls, stops and safety

Hyderabad to Vijayawada by road on NH 65: distance and driving time, the route through Suryapet and Kodad, FASTag tolls, stops, highway safety and when to go.

Hyderabad to Vijayawada by road is roughly 270 to 280 km on National Highway 65, and the drive usually takes four and a half to six hours. It is a four-lane divided highway through Choutuppal, Suryapet, Kodad, Jaggayyapeta and Nandigama, with three FASTag toll plazas on the way.

This guide covers the route, the towns, tolls, stops, the safety points that matter on this highway and when to go, as of November 2026. We have left out toll rates, because NHAI revises them; the FASTag app and the toll plaza boards show the current amount. Distances and times are ranges, because they depend on where in each city you start and finish.

How far is it and how long does the drive take?

Expect about 270 to 280 km and four and a half to six hours of driving, plus your stops. The biggest variable is getting out of Hyderabad: from the western suburbs you cross most of the city, while from LB Nagar or the ORR you are almost on the highway already.

What is the route from Hyderabad to Vijayawada?

Leave Hyderabad on the Vijayawada highway through LB Nagar and Hayathnagar, cross the Outer Ring Road near Pedda Amberpet, and stay on NH 65 all the way. In order, the main places are:

  1. LB Nagar and Hayathnagar, the slow, busy eastern edge of the city.
  2. Pedda Amberpet, where the highway meets the ORR. Joining from the ORR here avoids the city if you live near another ORR exit; see our Outer Ring Road guide.
  3. Choutuppal, with the Pantangi toll plaza, the first major toll.
  4. Narketpally, where roads branch off towards Nalgonda, followed by the Korlapahad toll plaza.
  5. Suryapet, roughly the halfway point.
  6. Kodad, the last large town in Telangana.
  7. Jaggayyapeta in Andhra Pradesh, with the Chillakallu toll plaza.
  8. Nandigama and Kanchikacherla, then Ibrahimpatnam and into Vijayawada across the approach to the city.

Navigation apps sometimes suggest a detour through Bhongir or Khammam when NH 65 is jammed. These are slower two-lane roads in places, so use them only if the main highway is badly blocked.

What should you know about tolls and FASTag?

All three main toll plazas take FASTag, and you should not travel without a working one. GMR built the four-lane highway and collected tolls from 2012 until mid-2024, when NHAI took over toll collection.

  • Without FASTag you pay more. Since 15 November 2025, under the National Highways Fee rules, a vehicle without a valid FASTag pays 1.25 times the toll if it pays by UPI and twice the toll in cash.
  • Annual pass. NHAI sells a FASTag annual pass for private cars, valid for one year or 200 toll crossings on national highways, whichever comes first. If you drive this highway often, compare it with paying per trip on the NHAI or Rajmargyatra channels.
  • Before you leave, check your FASTag balance and that the tag is active and linked to the right vehicle.

Where can you stop for food and fuel?

There are plenty of stops, and you rarely go long without a fuel station. In general terms:

  • Dhabas and highway restaurants line the stretch around Choutuppal, Narketpally and Suryapet, with Telangana and Andhra meals, tiffin in the morning and tea at all hours.
  • Fuel stations are frequent, and many have clean toilets and space to park. Larger ones near the towns are usually better lit at night.
  • Towns such as Suryapet and Kodad have more choice if you want to leave the highway, but turning across the median into a town adds time.
  • A good rhythm is a short break every two hours. Suryapet, near the halfway mark, is a natural place for a longer stop.

Park well clear of the carriageway when you stop, and do not pull up on the shoulder for tea at a roadside stall. Lorries and buses use the shoulder too.

How do you drive NH 65 safely?

Treat it as a fast highway with village traffic mixed in. The road is divided, but it passes many villages and junctions where people cross, and accidents on this highway are a frequent concern.

  • Keep left and overtake on the right. Slow lorries often sit in the right lane; do not undertake them on the left at speed.
  • Watch for wrong-side traffic. Two-wheelers, tractors and even cars sometimes come the wrong way on your side to reach a median opening.
  • Slow down at junctions and villages. Pedestrians, cattle and two-wheelers cross at median openings, especially near towns.
  • Follow the posted speed limits, which change along the route, and slow down well before toll plazas, where queues build fast.
  • Keep a long gap behind lorries and buses so you can see past them and stop in time.
  • Take breaks and do not drive tired. Microsleep on a straight, familiar highway is a real risk, especially after lunch and late at night.
  • Expect works zones. NHAI plans to widen most of NH 65 between the two cities to six lanes with service roads. As of November 2026 bids had been invited and work was expected to start in early 2027, so diversions are likely for some years.
Wear seat belts in every seat, front and back. If you break down, get everyone out on the side away from traffic and behind the barrier if there is one, and put the hazard lights and warning triangle on.

When is the best time to drive?

Early morning on a weekday is usually best: leave Hyderabad around dawn, clear the eastern suburbs before the morning rush and reach Vijayawada by late morning.

  • Avoid Sankranti weekends if you can. In January, huge numbers of people drive home to Andhra Pradesh. In January 2026 the Pantangi toll plaza handled over one lakh vehicles in a day, with delays of two to three hours reported at the toll booths, and the return journey a few days later was just as slow.
  • Long weekends and festivals such as Dasara bring similar, smaller peaks. Leaving a day early or very early in the morning helps.
  • Avoid late-night driving if you can. Lorries dominate the highway at night, and tiredness catches up with you.
  • Monsoon brings heavy rain and poor visibility; slow down and increase your gap.

Frequently asked questions

How long does it take to drive from Hyderabad to Vijayawada?

Usually about four and a half to six hours for roughly 270 to 280 km, depending on where you start in Hyderabad, traffic leaving the city and how long you stop. At festival times such as Sankranti it can take much longer.

Which highway connects Hyderabad and Vijayawada?

National Highway 65, a four-lane divided highway that runs from Hyderabad through Choutuppal, Suryapet and Kodad in Telangana and Jaggayyapeta and Nandigama in Andhra Pradesh to Vijayawada.

How many toll plazas are there between Hyderabad and Vijayawada?

There are three main toll plazas on NH 65: Pantangi near Choutuppal, Korlapahad between Narketpally and Suryapet and Chillakallu near Jaggayyapeta. NHAI took over toll collection in 2024, and payment is by FASTag.

Is the Hyderabad to Vijayawada highway being widened?

Yes. NHAI plans to widen most of NH 65 between the two cities to six lanes with service roads. As of November 2026 bids had been invited and work was expected to start in early 2027, so expect works zones over the next few years.
