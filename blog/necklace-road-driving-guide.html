Necklace Road driving guide: the lakeside loop round Hussain Sagar

Driving Necklace Road in Hyderabad: bends along Hussain Sagar, buses, U-turn openings, weekend crowds and evening traffic, plus how to rehearse the loop first.

Necklace Road is the lakeside road that sweeps round the western and northern shores of Hussain Sagar. It is a pleasant, mostly open drive with long bends and few junctions, but it asks for steady speed control and an eye on the median, where U-turns, buses and evening crowds can catch you out.

Here is what to expect on Necklace Road in Hyderabad, when it gets busy, the hazards worth knowing about, and how to rehearse the loop in Sawari before you drive it for real.

What Necklace Road is like

The road follows the curve of the lake from the Khairatabad side in the south-west, up the western shore and round towards the Secunderabad side in the north-east. On one side you have the water and the lakeside parks; on the other, for much of the way, the suburban railway line, with stations such as Khairatabad, Necklace Road, Sanjeevaiah Park and James Street.

Layout and lanes

For most of its length Necklace Road is a divided road with a median, about two lanes in each direction. That is narrower than Tank Bund Road across the lake, so overtaking a stopped bus or a slow auto needs more care. There are openings in the median for U-turns and crossings and a handful of signalled junctions.

The ground is mostly level, with only gentle rises and dips. What you notice instead are the bends: long, sweeping curves that follow the shoreline. None of them is sharp, but they are long enough that you cannot always see a stopped vehicle or a queue at a U-turn opening until you are well into the curve.

Along the way

Necklace Road is lined with places people come to spend an evening: Sanjeevaiah Park on the north-western shore, lakeside spaces such as People's Plaza and food stalls. From the road you get long views across Hussain Sagar to the Buddha statue and the Tank Bund side. It is a lovely drive, which is exactly why other drivers slow down to look.

When Necklace Road gets busy

Weekday mornings and evenings bring through traffic between the central offices and Secunderabad, as with most of Hyderabad traffic. But Necklace Road's busiest hours are often leisure hours: evenings, weekends and public holidays, when families, joggers and groups of friends head for the lakeside.

At those times you will find cars and two-wheelers pulling over to park, autos dropping people off, vendors near the kerb and many more people crossing. Special events and festivals around Hussain Sagar, Ganesh immersion above all, bring closures and diversions. Check Hyderabad Traffic Police advisories if you plan to drive at festival time.

For a first drive, early morning is usually best. The road is quieter, the light is soft and you can concentrate on your lines through the bends.

Hazards on Necklace Road

Bends and speed

Long curves invite you to carry speed, and the open lakeside makes the road feel faster than it is. Choose your speed before the bend, keep it steady through, and look as far round the curve as you can. If you need to brake, do it while the car is still straight. Follow the posted speed limit signs; do not take your cue from whoever is overtaking you.

U-turn openings

The median openings are where cars, autos and bikes swing round to go back the other way, sometimes slowly, sometimes from the right-hand lane without signalling, and occasionally from the left lane across your path. If you see a vehicle slowing in the right lane near an opening, assume it is about to turn. If you need to make a U-turn yourself, signal early, move to the right lane in good time and wait for a real gap. Our guide to U-turns in Hyderabad explains how to do it safely.

Buses and stopping vehicles

Buses pull in and out of stops, and with only two lanes each way, a stopped bus can take away half your road. Slow down as you approach a stop, check what is beside you before moving out, and be ready for the bus to pull out again without much warning.

People crossing to the lake

Visitors cross Necklace Road to reach the parks and the water, often in groups and often away from crossings. Children run ahead. At night, people in dark clothing are hard to see against the lake. Slow down near parks, stalls and parked cars, which can hide someone about to step out.

Speed breakers

There are speed breakers on and near the road, and some are poorly marked. Look for the yellow and black stripes, and for vehicles ahead dipping or braking. Slow down before the hump, not on it.

Practical tips for Necklace Road

  • Settle into the left lane unless you are overtaking or about to turn right. It keeps you out of the way of vehicles queuing for U-turns.
  • Leave a long gap. On a curving road with sudden stops, a bigger gap gives you time to react.
  • Signal every move. Indicate before lane changes, U-turns and when pulling in. Our guide to indicators and lane discipline covers the habits that matter most.
  • Park only where it is allowed. Stopping on the carriageway to look at the lake is dangerous for you and for the traffic behind.
  • Take extra care after dark. Headlights on, speed down, and expect pedestrians. See our night driving tips.

Practise Necklace Road in Sawari

Sawari's Necklace Road route is a 5.0 km drive round the western and northern shores of Hussain Sagar, finishing on the Secunderabad side. It has no turns to make, so it is a good second route after Tank Bund Road: longer, narrower and with more bends to judge.

  1. Start with light traffic. Get a feel for how the car carries speed into the bends. The physics model has tyre grip and weight transfer, so braking mid-curve makes the car run wide, much as a real one would.
  2. Follow the blue route band. It shows your lane on the route. Stay inside it and left of the centre line.
  3. Watch the speed limit sign at the bottom right of the screen. Staying more than a little over it for a few seconds is logged as a speeding fault.
  4. Use Q and E for the left and right indicators whenever you change lanes or pass a stopped bus.
  5. Turn the traffic up. Once you are comfortable, set traffic to Dense. Buses stop, bikes filter through gaps and the U-turn openings start to matter.
  6. Try the evening. Choose the evening or night lighting and switch on your headlights with L. The lakeside looks very different with the lamps on.

Press C to switch between the chase view and the driver's seat; the driver's seat is better for judging bends as you really will. The car has no mirrors, so drag the mouse to look around before you change lanes. At the end, the debrief lists every fault with advice: speeding, drifting right of the centre line, leaving the road, speed breakers taken too fast and collisions, including with the median or the lake edge.

If you miss your line and end up off the route, press R to return to it. You can drive the Necklace Road route now, free in Chrome, Edge or Firefox on a phone, tablet, laptop or desktop.

In the simulator vs the real road

The road's line, its lane counts, the median and the lake shore in Sawari come from OpenStreetMap, so the curves you practise are the real ones. Other details are estimated. Speed breakers, U-turn openings, stalls, parked vehicles, hoardings, most street lamps and many building facades are placed by rule where the map has no detail, so do not expect to find a hump or a stall in exactly the same spot on the real road. The speed limit shown in the game comes from map data; on the day, the posted sign is what counts.

Use the simulator to build habits: steady speed through bends, a good gap, early signals and an eye on the median. Then follow the real signs, signals and traffic police when you drive.

Frequently asked questions

Is Necklace Road easy to drive?

It is one of the gentler drives in central Hyderabad: a divided road with long, open bends and few junctions. The things to watch are buses, vehicles using the U-turn openings in the median, and people crossing to the lakeside in the evening.

Why is it called Necklace Road?

It curves round the shore of Hussain Sagar, and seen from above the road and its lights look like a necklace around the lake. That is the usual explanation, and the name has stuck.

When is Necklace Road crowded?

Evenings, weekends and holidays, when people come to the lakeside parks and food stalls. Weekday office hours also bring through traffic. Early mornings are usually the calmest time to drive it.
