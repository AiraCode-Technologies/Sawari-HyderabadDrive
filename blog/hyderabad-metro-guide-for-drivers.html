Hyderabad Metro guide for drivers: the three lines, driving under the viaducts and park-and-ride

Hyderabad Metro lines explained for people who drive: where the Red, Blue and Green lines run, driving under the viaducts, park-and-ride and Phase 2 plans.

The Hyderabad Metro has three lines, Red, Blue and Green, running about 69 km on viaducts over the city's busiest roads. If you drive in Hyderabad, you spend a lot of time underneath it, so it helps to know where the Hyderabad metro lines run, how to drive safely below them, and when it makes sense to leave the car and take the train.

The three Hyderabad metro lines

The first section opened in November 2017, and the three-line network was completed in February 2020. It was built by L&T Metro Rail (Hyderabad) under a concession with the state. In 2026 the Telangana government bought out L&T's stake, and the network is now under the state-owned Hyderabad Metro Rail Limited (HMRL).

  • The Red Line runs north-west to south-east, from Miyapur through Kukatpally, Ameerpet, Lakdikapul, Nampally and Malakpet to LB Nagar.
  • The Blue Line runs east to west, from Nagole through Uppal, Tarnaka, Secunderabad, Begumpet, Ameerpet, Jubilee Hills and Madhapur to HITEC City and Raidurg.
  • The Green Line is the short one, from JBS Parade Ground in Secunderabad through Musheerabad to MGBS.

There are three interchanges: Ameerpet (Red and Blue), MGBS (Red and Green) and Parade Ground (Blue and Green).

For a driver, the useful way to think of it is this: if your road has a concrete viaduct running down the middle, you are probably on a metro corridor, and that road will have stations, buses and crowds of pedestrians at regular intervals.

Driving under the metro viaducts

Most of the network runs on a viaduct above the middle of a main road, with the pillars in the median. That changes how the road below drives.

Pillars and the median

  • Pillars hide things. A row of thick pillars blocks your view of the opposite carriageway and of anyone stepping out from the median. Expect pedestrians, two-wheelers and even cattle to appear from between pillars, especially near stations and markets.
  • U-turns are under the viaduct. Many median openings and U-turns on metro roads sit between pillars. Vehicles waiting to turn may be partly hidden, and they often nose out slowly. Our guide to U-turns in Hyderabad covers how to take one safely.
  • The right-hand lane is narrower than it looks. Pillar bases and barriers can sit close to the fast lane. Keep a little space on your right and take care at night.

Stations

Each elevated station spans the road, with stairs, lifts and escalators coming down to the footpaths on both sides. Around every station you can expect:

  • Autos, cabs and share autos stopping in the left lane to pick up and drop off.
  • Buses pulling in at nearby stops.
  • People crossing the road to reach the stairs, often not at a crossing.
  • Two-wheelers parked along the kerb and pulling out.

Slow down as you approach a station, cover the brake and watch the left lane carefully. Our post on sharing the road with autos, buses and two-wheelers has more on reading their moves.

Light, rain and junctions

Under the deck the road is in shade by day and can be darker than the rest of the street at night, so pedestrians in dark clothes are harder to see. In heavy rain, water can collect where it runs off the viaduct. At big junctions where the metro crosses, the pillars and station structures can block your view of the signal and of traffic turning across, so approach slowly and look twice.

Never stop under a metro station to drop someone off in a live lane. Pull fully into the kerb where it is allowed, or use a proper pick-up point. Sudden stops near stations cause many rear-end bumps.

Combining car and metro

For many trips, the fastest option is to drive part of the way and take the metro for the rest, especially into the busiest parts of the city where parking is hard.

Park-and-ride

Some stations, especially at or near the ends of the lines, have parking areas for cars and two-wheelers. Stations in the city centre often have little or none. Capacity, charges and rules change, and popular car parks fill up early on weekdays, so check before you rely on one. The Hyderabad Metro Rail website is the place for current station information.

When it makes sense

  • Commuting into Ameerpet, Punjagutta, Begumpet or HITEC City at peak hours. Parking near offices and malls in these areas is scarce, and the roads below the viaduct are often slow.
  • Going to Secunderabad station or MGBS. Both are on the network, and both are busy places to drive into.
  • Events and festivals. When roads are closed or diverted, the metro often keeps running.

It makes less sense for trips well away from the lines, or when you are carrying heavy luggage, since many stations involve long stairs and walks.

Phase 2 and the airport metro

Phase 2 is the plan to extend the network, and its status changes often, so this is the position at the time of writing (November 2026).

  • The state approved a first set of corridors, often called Phase 2A, of about 76 km in late 2024 and sent it to the Centre. It includes extensions to the existing lines and a new line from Nagole to Rajiv Gandhi International Airport at Shamshabad.
  • A further set of corridors, Phase 2B, was approved by the state in 2025.
  • In May 2026 the Centre said it was ready to give in-principle support and to share costs equally with the state. At the time of writing (November 2026), final central approval for the first stage had been reported but not officially confirmed, so treat any completion date as provisional.
  • No Phase 2 line is open, and timelines for metro projects often slip.

An earlier plan for an airport express line from Raidurg, through the Financial District, to the airport was dropped after the 2023 change of government in favour of the new Nagole route. Until a line opens, the airport is reached by road, mostly via the PV Narasimha Rao Expressway or the Outer Ring Road. See our ORR guide and our post on Hyderabad's flyovers and underpasses.

Practising metro-corridor roads in Sawari

Frequently asked questions

How many metro lines are there in Hyderabad?

Three: the Red Line from Miyapur to LB Nagar, the Blue Line from Nagole to Raidurg, and the Green Line from JBS Parade Ground to MGBS. Together they cover about 69 km, almost all of it elevated.

Where can I change between Hyderabad metro lines?

Ameerpet links the Red and Blue lines, MG Bus Station (MGBS) links the Red and Green lines, and Parade Ground links the Blue and Green lines.

Is there a metro to Hyderabad airport?

Not yet. As of November 2026 a Nagole to airport line is part of the Phase 2 plans, but it has not been built. The earlier plan for an airport express line from Raidurg was dropped in favour of the new proposal.

Can I park my car at a Hyderabad metro station?

Some stations, especially at or near the ends of lines, have parking, but many in the city centre have little or none. Capacity and charges change, so check at the station or with Hyderabad Metro Rail before relying on it.
