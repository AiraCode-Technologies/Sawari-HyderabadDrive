Hyderabad to Bengaluru by road: the NH 44 drive via Kurnool and Anantapur

Hyderabad to Bengaluru by road on NH 44: distance and driving time, the route via Kurnool and Anantapur, FASTag tolls, stops, night driving cautions and more.

Hyderabad to Bengaluru by road is roughly 560 to 610 km on National Highway 44, and the drive usually takes nine to eleven hours with short stops. The route runs south past Jadcherla to Kurnool, through Gooty and Anantapur in Andhra Pradesh, and into Karnataka past Bagepalli, Chikkaballapur and Devanahalli.

It is one of the longest drives people regularly make from Hyderabad, and most of it is a four-lane divided highway. This guide covers the route, tolls, stops, the cautions that matter most on a trip this long, and the alternatives, as of November 2026. We have left out toll rates, which NHAI revises, and give distances and times as ranges because they depend on where you start and finish.

How far is it and how long does it take?

Expect about 560 to 610 km and nine to eleven hours door to door, including short breaks. Starting near Shamshabad and ending in north Bengaluru is at the short end; starting in west Hyderabad and ending in south Bengaluru adds an hour or more of city driving.

What is the route via Kurnool and Anantapur?

Stay on NH 44 the whole way. The main places, in order:

  1. Shamshabad, past the airport. If you live near the ORR, join NH 44 at the Shamshabad interchange to avoid the city; see our Outer Ring Road guide.
  2. Shadnagar and Jadcherla, the first stretch through Telangana.
  3. The Krishna river crossing and the approach to the state border.
  4. Kurnool, across the Tungabhadra in Andhra Pradesh, a little over a third of the way and the first big city.
  5. Dhone and Gooty, through dry, open country.
  6. Anantapur, the other large city on the route.
  7. Penukonda, then the Karnataka border near Kodikonda.
  8. Bagepalli and Chikkaballapur, where the road climbs gently towards the Bengaluru plateau.
  9. Devanahalli, past Kempegowda International Airport, then into the city via Hebbal.

The last stretch from the airport into Bengaluru is often slow, especially on weekday evenings. Our comparison of Hyderabad and Bengaluru traffic explains why.

What should you know about tolls and FASTag?

There are several toll plazas between the two cities, all on FASTag, so check your balance before you leave.

  • Without FASTag you pay more. Since 15 November 2025, a vehicle without a valid FASTag pays 1.25 times the toll by UPI and twice the toll in cash on national highways.
  • Annual pass. The NHAI FASTag annual pass for private cars covers one year or 200 toll crossings on national highways, whichever comes first. On a route with this many plazas, a return trip uses a lot of crossings, so check whether it suits you on NHAI's channels.
  • Toll queues are longest around long weekends and festivals, and at the plazas nearest each city.

Where should you stop?

Plan two or three real breaks, not just fuel stops, because ten hours at the wheel is tiring. In general terms:

  • Around Jadcherla for an early breakfast or tea if you leave before dawn.
  • Kurnool for a proper meal and a rest, since it is a large city with plenty of choice just off the highway.
  • Anantapur for a second meal or a long break.
  • Fuel stations along the whole route, many with toilets and food courts. Fill up before the long, emptier stretches between towns.

Carry water, especially in summer, when the Rayalaseema stretch around Kurnool and Anantapur gets very hot. Check tyre pressures, coolant and the spare before you set off.

Is it safe to drive NH 44, especially at night?

You can drive it at night, but daytime is safer and less tiring. NH 44 carries heavy lorry and sleeper-bus traffic after dark, and night adds hazards you do not see by day:

  • Unlit vehicles. Tractors, carts and some two-wheelers run without proper lights, and broken-down lorries are often parked on the carriageway with little warning.
  • People and animals. Villagers cross at junctions and cattle wander onto the road, especially near towns.
  • Glare. Oncoming high beams across the median dazzle you; look towards the left edge of your lane, not at the lights.
  • Tiredness. The straight stretches through Rayalaseema invite microsleep, most of all between about 2 and 5 am.

Night crashes here can be severe. In October 2025 a Hyderabad to Bengaluru sleeper bus caught fire after hitting a motorcycle on NH 44 near Kurnool, and around 20 people died. It is a reminder to keep a long gap, slow down for anything unclear in your headlights and never drive while drowsy.

If you must drive at night, share the driving, stop every two hours, keep to the posted speed limits and use dipped headlights when other vehicles approach. Everyone, front and back, should wear a seat belt.

Habits for the whole drive

By day or night, drive it as a fast divided highway that still has local traffic:

  • Keep left and overtake on the right, with your indicator on, and move back left once you are well past.
  • Expect wrong-side traffic near median openings and villages, mainly two-wheelers and tractors.
  • Slow down through towns and junctions, and well before toll plazas.
  • Follow the posted speed limits, which change between states and stretches.
  • Watch for works. NHAI has plans to upgrade parts of NH 44 between the two cities, including bypasses and underpasses around Kurnool, and wider sections near Bengaluru. As of November 2026 several were at the planning or tender stage, so expect diversions in the coming years.

Leaving Hyderabad early, around dawn, usually gets you through Kurnool by mid-morning and into Bengaluru before the evening rush.

What are the alternatives to driving?

If you do not need a car at the other end, other options are often less tiring:

  • Flights are the quickest, with many daily flights between the two airports.
  • Train. The Kacheguda to Yesvantpur Vande Bharat Express, running since September 2023 via Mahbubnagar, Kurnool City and Anantapur, takes a little over eight hours. Many overnight trains also run.
  • Bus. Government and private sleeper buses run overnight.
  • Split the drive. An overnight stop in Kurnool or Anantapur turns one long day into two easy ones.

Frequently asked questions

How long does it take to drive from Hyderabad to Bengaluru?

Usually about nine to eleven hours for roughly 560 to 610 km, including short stops. The open highway is quick; most of the variation comes from leaving Hyderabad, entering Bengaluru and how long you stop.

Which highway goes from Hyderabad to Bengaluru?

National Highway 44, the north-south highway, runs from Hyderabad past Shamshabad and Jadcherla, through Kurnool, Gooty and Anantapur in Andhra Pradesh, and on through Bagepalli, Chikkaballapur and Devanahalli into Bengaluru.

Is it safe to drive from Hyderabad to Bengaluru at night?

It is possible, but daytime is safer. At night the highway carries many lorries and sleeper buses, and you meet unlit vehicles, pedestrians and animals, while tiredness builds on the long straight stretches. If you must drive at night, share the driving and stop every two hours.

What are the alternatives to driving from Hyderabad to Bengaluru?

Flights are the fastest. By rail, the Kacheguda to Yesvantpur Vande Bharat Express via Kurnool and Anantapur takes a little over eight hours, and many overnight trains and buses also run between the two cities.
