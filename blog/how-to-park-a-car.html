How to park a car: reverse bay parking, parallel parking, angle parking and slopes

How to park a car step by step: reverse into a bay, parallel park, angle park, and park safely on a slope with the wheels turned, handbrake on and in gear.

To park a car well, go slowly, use reference points on the car to judge where its corners are, and look all round before and during every movement. Reversing into a bay is usually safer than driving in forwards, parallel parking is a set sequence of steering moves, and on a slope you turn the wheels, apply the handbrake and leave the car in gear or in Park.

Parking is the manoeuvre that makes new drivers most anxious, especially with people watching. The good news is that it is a routine, not a talent. This guide gives the steps for the common types, assuming a car with the driver on the right and parking on the left, as in India. Practise in an empty car park with an instructor or a licensed driver beside you.

What are the basics of parking any car?

Slow speed, all-round observation and reference points are the three things that make every kind of parking easier.

  • Go very slowly. In a manual, use the clutch at the bite point; in an automatic, let the car creep on the brake. Slow speed gives you time to correct.
  • Look all round before you start and keep looking while you move: mirrors, over both shoulders and through the rear window. Pedestrians and two-wheelers move into car parks without warning.
  • Signal to show others what you are doing.
  • Stop and correct. If it is going wrong, stop, pull forward and try again. Nobody is scored on parking in one go in real life.
  • Get out and look while you are learning, to link what you saw from the seat with where the car really is.

Using reference points without mirrors or cameras

A reference point is a part of the car that lines up with something outside, such as a bay line or the kerb, when the car is in the right place. Every car is different, so find your own with cones or chalk lines in an empty space:

  • Pull up until a line on the ground meets a point on the bonnet or the bottom corner of the windscreen, then get out and check where the bumper is. That view becomes your "front bumper on the line" reference.
  • Do the same for the left side: drive along a line until it appears to meet a point on the dashboard. That is your "left wheels on the line" view, useful for getting close to the kerb.
  • Looking back over your left shoulder, notice where a line or the kerb appears in the rear side window when the rear wheel is level with it.

Mirrors and parking cameras are a help, not a replacement. Learn the reference points first and you can park any car, including an old one with none of the gadgets.

How do you reverse into a parking bay?

Drive past the bay, stop when your car is two or three bays beyond it, then reverse slowly in while steering towards it and straighten up as the car lines up with the bay.

  1. Position. Drive slowly along the row about one to one-and-a-half metres from the parked cars or bay ends. Signal towards the bay.
  2. Stop when your rear bumper has passed the bay and the bay's far line is roughly level with your shoulder, or about two bay lines past the space. Adjust for your car.
  3. Observe. Check all round, especially behind and on the side away from the bay, where your front will swing out.
  4. Reverse slowly and steer fully towards the bay. Watch the nearest bay line in your mirror or over your shoulder as the car swings round.
  5. Straighten up when the car is parallel with the bay lines, by turning the wheel back the same amount you turned it.
  6. Reverse straight back until the front is inside the bay. Stop before the rear wheels reach the kerb or the end line.
  7. Secure the car: handbrake on, gear in first or reverse (manual) or Park (automatic), engine off.
If you are crooked, drive forward a little to straighten, then reverse again. Small corrections at walking pace are better than one big swing.

How do you parallel park?

Parallel parking is a repeatable sequence: line up with the car in front, reverse until your rear wheels are level with its bumper, steer in at an angle, then steer the other way to bring the front in.

You need a space roughly one-and-a-half times your car's length while you are learning.

  1. Signal left and stop alongside the car in front of the space, level with it or slightly ahead, about half a metre to a metre away.
  2. Check all round, especially for traffic behind and two-wheelers on your left.
  3. Reverse slowly and straight until your rear wheels are level with the other car's rear bumper.
  4. Steer fully left and keep reversing slowly until your car is at about 45 degrees to the kerb. A useful check: the kerb appears in the left mirror or the left rear window at the corner.
  5. Straighten the wheel and reverse in a straight line until your front bumper clears the rear of the car in front.
  6. Steer fully right and keep reversing slowly. The front swings in towards the kerb.
  7. Straighten up as the car becomes parallel with the kerb, then move forward or back to leave room for the cars on both sides.

Check the parking is legal before you start: the Motor Vehicles (Driving) Regulations, 2017 list places where you must not park, including footpaths, pedestrian crossings, near junctions and bus stops, in front of gates and where a continuous yellow line runs along the kerb. How do you angle park?

Angle parking is the easiest: approach so the car can turn into the slanted bay in one smooth movement, and steer in once your mirror passes the near line.

  1. Drive along the row in the direction the bays slant, keeping a little away from the parked cars to give yourself room to turn.
  2. Signal and slow to walking pace.
  3. Start turning when your front is roughly level with the first line of the bay, or your mirror is just past it.
  4. Steer into the bay, then straighten up when the car is aligned with the lines.
  5. Stop before the kerb or wheel stop, then secure the car.

When leaving, reverse out very slowly, because you cannot see along the row until the back of the car is out. Stop and look often.

How do you park safely on a slope?

Turn the front wheels so the car would roll into the kerb or off the road, apply the handbrake firmly and leave the car in gear or in Park.

SituationFront wheelsManual gearbox
Downhill, with a kerbTurned left, towards the kerbReverse gear
Uphill, with a kerbTurned right, away from the kerb, so the tyre rests against it if the car rolls backFirst gear
Uphill or downhill, no kerbTurned left, towards the edge of the roadFirst uphill, reverse downhill
  1. Stop close to the left edge and keep the footbrake pressed.
  2. Turn the wheels as in the table, then let the car roll gently until the tyre touches the kerb, if there is one.
  3. Apply the handbrake firmly.
  4. In a manual, select the gear in the table before switching off. In an automatic, apply the handbrake first, then select Park, so the car's weight sits on the brake rather than the gearbox.

Hyderabad has more slopes than people expect, for example the climb on Banjara Hills Road No. 1. Our guide to driving an automatic in India explains Park and the handbrake on an automatic.

Is parking part of the RTA driving test?

Parking-type manoeuvres often are, but the details vary by office and change over time, so check with your driving school or the RTA.

Some Telangana RTA offices use test tracks with markers, and manoeuvres such as reversing or parking in a marked space may be included. We do not list a format here, because we cannot confirm one that applies everywhere. Ask your instructor what your office tests and practise exactly that. Frequently asked questions

Is it better to reverse into a parking space or drive in forwards?

Reversing in is usually safer, because you drive out forwards with a clear view of passing cars and people. You reverse into the space at low speed with a good view of the bay lines, rather than reversing blind into a busy lane later.

How do I parallel park step by step?

Stop alongside the car in front of the space, about half a metre to a metre away. Reverse slowly until your rear wheels are level with its back bumper, steer fully left until the car is at about 45 degrees, straighten up, then steer fully right as your front clears its bumper, and straighten up close to the kerb.

Which way should I turn my wheels when parking on a hill?

In India, where you park on the left: facing downhill, turn the front wheels left towards the kerb. Facing uphill with a kerb, turn them right, away from the kerb, so the tyre rests against it if the car rolls back. With no kerb, turn them left towards the road edge either way. Always apply the handbrake.
