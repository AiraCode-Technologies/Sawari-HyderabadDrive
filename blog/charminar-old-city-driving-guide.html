Driving to Charminar: an Old City driving guide to Charminar Road and Pathergatti

Driving near Charminar in Hyderabad's Old City: narrow one-way streets, the circle round the monument, Pathergatti, parking, crowds and how to practise first.

Driving near Charminar is the most demanding short drive in Hyderabad: narrow one-way streets, a circle round the monument, shoppers and handcarts at the kerb, and autos and two-wheelers in every gap. It is very manageable at walking pace and with patience, but it rewards preparation, and it is no place for a first drive.

This guide covers the Old City roads around the Charminar and Pathergatti, when they are busiest, what catches drivers out, where parking fits in, and how to rehearse the route in Sawari before you go.

What the roads around Charminar are like

The Charminar, built in the late sixteenth century under the Qutb Shahi dynasty, stands at the centre of the Old City, where the main streets meet. The Mecca Masjid is close by to the south-west, the bangle shops of Laad Bazaar run off to the west, and to the north the road passes Gulzar Houz and continues as Pathergatti, with its long arcade of stone-fronted shops.

Layout and lanes

Around the monument the streets are mostly one-way, and many are only one or two cars wide once you allow for parked two-wheelers, shop goods and handcarts. Traffic moves round the Charminar itself rather than past it, so you need to know which exit you want before you arrive. Lane markings are scarce; your real lane is whatever space is left between the kerb and the vehicles beside you.

Pathergatti Road is wider and busier, a main shopping street lined with arched shopfronts. The ground falls gently as you head north towards the river Musi, so on a crowded street you will be covering the brake more than the accelerator.

One-way rules

Because so many streets are one-way, the direct route is often not the legal one. You may need to loop through side lanes to reach a street you can see straight ahead. One-way arrangements around the Old City can also change with works, events and traffic management, so follow the signs on the day rather than an old memory of the route.

When Charminar and the Old City are busiest

The Old City wakes later than the rest of Hyderabad. Early morning, before shutters go up, is usually the calmest time to drive. From late morning the shops open, deliveries arrive and the crowds build, and by evening the streets around the Charminar are packed.

Weekends and holidays bring tourists as well as shoppers. Festival seasons are busier still. During Ramzan the area round the Charminar becomes a night market, and the streets are full late into the night. Muharram, Bonalu and other festivals bring processions and diversions. If you need to drive at these times, check Hyderabad Traffic Police advisories first, or leave the car behind.

Hazards when driving near Charminar

People everywhere

Pedestrians are the main traffic here. Shoppers step off the footpath to get round goods and parked bikes, people cross wherever they like, and visitors stop in the road to take photographs of the monument. Drive at a speed where you could stop within a car's length, and keep looking at the footpaths as well as the road.

Two-wheelers and autos

Bikes and scooters squeeze past on both sides and cut in front at the circle. Autos stop dead to pick up and drop off. Hold a steady, predictable line; do not make sudden moves to block a bike, and do not assume the vehicle ahead will keep going. Our guide to sharing the road with autos, buses and two-wheelers has more on this.

Stalls, handcarts and parked vehicles

Goods spill out of shops, fruit and flower carts stop at the kerb, and parked two-wheelers line both sides. That narrows the road and hides people about to step out. Keep a gap of at least a door's width from parked vehicles where you can, and watch for a foot or a wheel appearing between them.

The circle round the monument

Traffic round the Charminar behaves like a busy roundabout with little lane discipline. Approach slowly, give way to vehicles already going round, signal your exit, and expect bikes to pass on your left as you leave. The general rules in our guide to driving roundabouts in India apply, but here everything happens at walking pace.

Speed breakers

Old City streets have many speed breakers, and not all are painted. Watch for vehicles ahead dipping and take every hump slowly and straight on.

Charminar parking and practical tips

  • Think about whether to drive at all. For a visit to the Charminar, many people find it easier to come by auto, cab or bus and walk the last part.
  • Park only where it is signed. Space near the monument is very limited. Never park on the carriageway, across a lane or in front of a shop entrance; it blocks traffic and you may be towed or fined.
  • Know your route before you set off. With one-way streets, a wrong turn can cost you a long loop. Check it beforehand rather than reading a phone in traffic.
  • Go slowly and keep going. A steady crawl is easier for everyone to read than stop-start jerks.
  • Use the horn sparingly. A short tap to say you are there is enough; long blasts do not clear a crowded street.
  • Take an experienced driver with you the first time you drive here.

Practise the Charminar route in Sawari

Sawari's Charminar and Pathergatti route is a 2.1 km drive up Charminar Road from the south, round the Charminar itself, then north to Pathergatti Road. It is the hardest of the five routes: nine turns in two kilometres, including a short loop through side lanes because of the way the one-way streets are mapped.

  1. Drive it with no traffic first. Set traffic to None and learn the turns. Follow the blue route band, which always shows the legal direction on one-way streets, and listen for the spoken directions.
  2. Signal every turn. Press Q for left or E for right well before the turn. The indicator cancels itself afterwards. Turning without one is logged in your debrief.
  3. Use the driver's seat view. Press C to switch from the chase view. Judging gaps from the driver's seat is harder, and closer to the real thing. Drag the mouse to look round corners and at the circle before you join it.
  4. Add traffic. Raise the traffic level step by step. At Dense, autos, scooters and motorbikes crowd the streets and the circle round the monument gets busy.
  5. Try it at night. The night setting lights the streets with lamps and lit windows, and every vehicle has head and tail lamps. Switch your own headlights on with L.

The debrief at the end gives you a score and every fault with advice: collisions with vehicles, pedestrians, walls or buildings, going the wrong way on a one-way street, running a red light, taking speed breakers too fast, leaving the road and missed indicators. If you take a wrong turn, press R to return to the route.

If this route feels too much at first, that is normal. Build up on Tank Bund Road and Necklace Road, then come back. You can start the Charminar drive here, free on a phone, tablet, laptop or desktop.

In the simulator vs the real road

The streets, their widths and their one-way directions in Sawari come from OpenStreetMap, and the Charminar is modelled as a landmark at its real position. Much of the street life is estimated. Stalls, parked two-wheelers, hoardings, speed breakers, most street lamps and many facades are placed by rule to look typical of the Old City, not copied from the real street. The game also cannot show the current vehicle restrictions around the monument, which can change.

So practise the skills, not the exact street: slow speed, wide awareness, early signals and patience at the circle. On the day, follow the real signs and the traffic police.

Frequently asked questions

Can you drive right up to the Charminar?

Vehicle access around the monument has been restricted in recent years as parts of the area were made pedestrian-friendly, and the rules can change. Follow the signs and the traffic police on the day, and expect to park some distance away and walk.

Is there parking near Charminar?

Space close to the monument is very limited and is often taken early, especially at weekends and during festivals. Use only places signed for parking, never the carriageway or a shop front, or come by auto, cab or bus and walk the last stretch.

What is the best time to drive in the Old City?

Early morning, before the shops open, is usually the calmest. Late mornings, evenings, weekends, festival days and Ramzan nights are much busier, with heavy crowds on foot around the Charminar.

Is the Charminar route hard for beginners?

It is the hardest of Sawari's five routes: narrow one-way streets, a circle round the monument and nine turns in about two kilometres. Practise the easier lakeside routes first, and drive the real streets with an experienced driver beside you.
