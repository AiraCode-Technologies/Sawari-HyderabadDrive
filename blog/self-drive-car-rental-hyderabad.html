Self-drive car rental in Hyderabad: how it works, from documents to returning the car

How self-drive car rental in Hyderabad works: documents, security deposits, fuel policy, checking the car, FASTag tolls, insurance, damage and returning it.

Self-drive car rental in Hyderabad works much as it does elsewhere: you book a car through an app or agency, show your licence and ID, pay the rent and usually a refundable deposit, and return the car with the agreed fuel, paying any tolls, fines and damage afterwards. The details that cost people money are the fuel policy, the damage terms and the check-out inspection, so read those closely.

This guide explains the process in general terms as of November 2026. It does not compare prices or recommend any company. Rental firms in Hyderabad range from app-based services, including marketplaces such as Zoomcar where individual hosts list their cars, to local agencies and fleet operators. Their terms differ, so treat this as a checklist rather than a rulebook.

How does self-drive rental work in Hyderabad?

You book a car for a set period, collect it from a hub or have it delivered, drive it yourself and hand it back. The rent may be charged by the hour or the day, with or without a kilometre limit, and with or without fuel included.

Legitimate self-drive rental cars are registered as commercial vehicles, which in India usually means a black number plate with yellow characters. A private white-plate car offered for self-drive hire is a warning sign worth asking about.

What documents do you need?

Every firm asks for a valid driving licence that covers cars, and most also want a government photo ID such as Aadhaar or a passport. Some ask for address proof or a selfie and document upload in the app before the booking is confirmed.

  • Your licence must be valid on every day of the booking. An expired licence can void the insurance even if the firm did not notice.
  • Visitors from abroad are typically asked for their home licence, an International Driving Permit and a passport. Some firms rent only to Indian licence holders. See our guide to driving in Hyderabad for NRIs and visitors.
  • Only the named driver may drive. If someone else will share the driving, add them to the booking; an unlisted driver usually means no cover.

How do deposits, fuel and kilometre limits work?

These three set the real cost of a rental, so understand them before you pay.

Deposits

A refundable deposit is common, held on a card or paid by UPI or bank transfer. Firms deduct fuel shortfalls, tolls, challans, late fees and damage before returning it. Ask how many days the refund takes, and keep the receipt.

Fuel policy

  • Same to same. You return the car with the fuel level you received. Photograph the gauge at pick-up and refuel near the drop point.
  • Fuel included. The price includes fuel for a set distance, and you pay per extra kilometre. Do not refuel at your own cost unless the firm tells you to.
  • Electric cars. The battery level works like fuel. Check the charge at pick-up and know where you will charge. Our guide to electric cars in Hyderabad covers charging.

Kilometre limits

Some plans include unlimited kilometres; others cap the distance and charge for extra. A weekend trip out of the city on a capped plan can cost much more than you expect, so check before planning a long drive.

What should you check before driving away?

Inspect the car properly, with the rental staff or host if possible, and record it. Most disputes come down to whether a scratch was there before you.

  1. Walk round with your phone recording. Film every panel, bumper, wheel and the windscreen in daylight, with the number plate in shot. Point out existing damage and make sure it is noted in the app or the form.
  2. Check the tyres and the spare. Look for bald patches and cuts, and confirm there is a spare, a jack and a wheel spanner.
  3. Check the papers. The registration certificate, insurance and a valid pollution certificate (PUC) should be in the car or available digitally.
  4. Test the basics. Lights, indicators, horn, wipers, brakes, air conditioning and the handbrake.
  5. Note the fuel and odometer on camera.
  6. Check the FASTag. Make sure a tag is fixed to the windscreen and ask whether it is active.
  7. Save the helpline number and ask what to do in a breakdown or accident.
If you are not used to the model, spend five minutes in the car park finding the gears, lights, wipers and hazard switch before you join traffic. If it is an automatic and you normally drive a manual, our guide to driving an automatic car helps.

Who pays for tolls, fines and damage?

You do: tolls and fines follow the car, the rental firm passes them to you after the trip, and most firms make you liable for damage up to a set amount.

FASTag, tolls and challans

  • FASTag. FASTag is the normal way to pay tolls on national highways and the Outer Ring Road. On a rental, tolls are paid from the car's tag and then billed to you. If the tag is not working, the toll plaza may charge more than the normal rate for paying another way.
  • E-challans. Hyderabad issues many traffic fines from cameras, linked to the number plate. They can arrive weeks after you return the car, and the firm will charge them to the driver who had the car at that time. You can look up a vehicle's pending challans on the Telangana e-challan portal.
  • Parking. Parking charges and towing fees during your rental are yours to pay.

What happens if the car is damaged?

All rental cars carry the insurance the law requires, but that does not mean you pay nothing after damage. Some firms sell extra protection that lowers the amount you are liable for.

  • Read the exclusions. Tyres, glass, the underbody, interior damage, driving on unpaved roads and driving under the influence are often excluded from the cover.
  • After an accident, stop safely, make sure no one is hurt, call the firm's helpline and photograph the scene. For serious incidents, the insurer will usually need a police report.
  • Do not arrange your own repairs or towing without the firm's approval, or you may end up paying for them.

How do you return the car?

Return it on time, with the agreed fuel or charge, at the agreed place, and record it again.

  • Allow for traffic. Late returns are usually charged by the hour. In the evening peak, the IT corridor and the central roads can add an hour to a short trip; see our guide to Hyderabad traffic hotspots and peak hours.
  • Film the car again at drop-off, including the fuel gauge and odometer.
  • Remove your belongings and any FASTag or parking receipts you need.
  • Check the final bill and the deposit refund. Query any deduction you do not understand with the photos and video you took.

Frequently asked questions

What documents do I need for a self-drive car in Hyderabad?

Usually a valid driving licence, a government photo ID such as Aadhaar or a passport, and sometimes address proof. Foreign licence holders are typically asked for an International Driving Permit and a passport. Each rental firm sets its own list, so check before you book.

Is a security deposit required for self-drive cars?

Many rental firms take a refundable deposit, while some offer bookings without one, often at a higher price or with conditions. Deposits are usually returned after the trip, minus any fuel, toll, challan or damage charges.

Who pays tolls and traffic fines on a rental car?

You do. Tolls are normally paid from the car's FASTag and charged to you afterwards, and e-challans issued during your booking are passed on to you, sometimes weeks later.

Can I take a Hyderabad rental car to another state?

Many firms allow outstation trips, but some charge extra, cap the distance or restrict certain states. Ask before you leave, because driving outside the allowed area can void the cover.
