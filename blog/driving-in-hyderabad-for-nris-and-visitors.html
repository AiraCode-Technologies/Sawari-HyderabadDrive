Driving in Hyderabad for NRIs and visitors: licences, permits, rentals and what feels different

Driving in Hyderabad as an NRI or foreign visitor: International Driving Permits, foreign and Indian licences, rentals, insurance and left-hand traffic.

Yes, NRIs and foreign visitors can drive in Hyderabad, but not on a foreign domestic licence alone: you need your home licence together with an International Driving Permit (IDP) issued in your home country, or a valid Indian driving licence. Traffic keeps to the left, and the biggest adjustment is the density and mix of vehicles rather than the rules themselves.

This guide covers the paperwork first, then renting, insurance and what driving here actually feels like. Rules are as we understand them in November 2026. Licensing is handled by the Telangana Transport Department (the RTA) and national rules are set by the Ministry of Road Transport and Highways, so check with them before you rely on anything here for a long stay.

India drives on the left. Cars are right-hand drive, and most rental cars at the budget end are manual, though automatics are increasingly common.

Can a foreigner drive in Hyderabad?

Yes, with the right documents. The Ministry of Road Transport and Highways has stated in its published answers that an NRI or foreigner cannot drive in India merely on the basis of a foreign domestic licence. The accepted route for a visitor is an International Driving Permit, carried with the original home licence it translates.

What an International Driving Permit is

An IDP is a booklet that translates your home licence into a standard format recognised under the 1949 Geneva Convention on Road Traffic, to which India is a party. It is not a licence on its own: it is only valid alongside your home licence.

  • Get it before you travel. An IDP must be issued in the country that issued your licence. The Telangana RTA issues IDPs only to residents holding Indian licences, so it cannot give one to a visitor.
  • Check the format. Some countries issue permits under more than one convention. Ask your issuing body for the one that covers India.
  • Mind the time limit. An IDP is generally treated as valid for driving in India for up to one year, and never beyond its own expiry or the expiry of your home licence.
  • Carry everything. Keep the IDP, the original home licence and your passport with you whenever you drive.

Can an NRI drive in India on an Indian licence?

Yes. A valid Indian driving licence works in India regardless of where you live. The problem for many NRIs is that the licence has quietly expired while they were abroad.

  • Check the validity date on your card or in DigiLocker before you land.
  • Renew online through Parivahan Sarathi, choosing the state and RTA that issued it. The longer a licence has been expired, the more the RTA may ask of you, including a fresh test after a long lapse, so do not leave it.
  • Old paper licences may need to be converted to a smart card or brought onto the national database before you can renew. Contact the issuing RTA if Sarathi cannot find yours.

Note that an Indian licence holder cannot drive in India on a foreign licence and IDP instead; the IDP system is for driving outside the country that issued your licence.

How do I get an Indian driving licence as a foreign national?

If you are living in Hyderabad rather than visiting, plan to get an Indian licence. India does not simply swap a foreign licence for an Indian one in most cases, so expect the normal process: a learner's licence (LLR) first, then a driving test at an RTA unit.

  • Documents. Expect to show your passport, a valid visa or residence permit for a longer stay, proof of your address in Hyderabad, photographs and your foreign licence. A short tourist visa is not suitable for this.
  • Apply online. Applications start on Parivahan Sarathi, where you book the learner's test and later the driving test.
  • Learner's licence. It is valid for six months, and you can take the driving test after the waiting period. Our guide to getting a driving licence in Hyderabad walks through every step.

Rules for foreign applicants differ in detail between states and have been under review nationally in 2026, with a draft proposal for a separate licence format for foreign nationals. Confirm the current document list with your RTA before you book.

Can I rent a car in Hyderabad as a visitor?

Usually yes, but each rental company sets its own rules, and they tend to be stricter than the law. Many self-drive firms ask for an Indian licence, or a foreign licence with an IDP and a passport, and set a minimum age. Some do not rent to foreign licence holders at all, so ask before you book.

Two other options suit many visitors better. A car with a driver is widely available and removes the stress of learning the city. Ride-hailing cabs and autos cover most trips inside the city. Our guide to self-drive car rental in Hyderabad explains deposits, fuel, FASTag and damage checks if you do rent.

What about insurance?

Every vehicle on Indian roads must have at least third-party insurance under the Motor Vehicles Act. A rental car or a family car will already have a policy, but you still need to check that it covers you.

  • Rental cars. Read what the rental cover includes, the amount you pay in the event of damage, and what is excluded, such as tyres, glass or driving on unpaved roads.
  • A relative's car. Motor policies generally cover any driver with a valid licence, but a claim can fail if your licence or permit is not valid in India. Get your papers right first.
  • Travel insurance. Many travel policies exclude or limit driving abroad. Check yours.

What feels different about driving in Hyderabad?

The rules are recognisable; the traffic is not. Visitors from countries with strict lane discipline usually find these the biggest adjustments:

  • Keeping left. If you are used to the right, the hardest moments are turning onto an empty road and leaving a car park. Say "left, left" to yourself at every turn for the first few days.
  • Two-wheelers everywhere. Motorcycles and scooters pass on both sides and fill every gap. Check mirrors constantly and change direction gently. Our guide to sharing the road with autos, buses and two-wheelers helps.
  • U-turns instead of right turns. On many main roads you go past the junction and turn back at a median opening. See U-turns in Hyderabad.
  • Free left turns. At some junctions you may turn left on red where a sign or arrow allows it. Never block that lane if you are going straight.
  • The horn. It is used as a "here I am" signal more than a rebuke. A short tap is normal; long blasts are not.
  • Speed breakers. Humps appear on main roads as well as side streets, often unmarked. Slow early.
  • Enforcement by camera. Hyderabad issues many fines as e-challans from cameras, linked to the vehicle number. With a rental, the firm will pass them on to you.
Start with a quiet weekend morning on a wide road, such as Tank Bund or Necklace Road around Hussain Sagar, before trying the IT corridor at rush hour or the Old City lanes.

Frequently asked questions

Can a foreigner drive in Hyderabad with a foreign licence?

Not on a foreign domestic licence alone. The Ministry of Road Transport and Highways says an NRI or foreigner cannot drive in India merely on a foreign licence. Visitors normally carry their home licence together with an International Driving Permit issued in their home country, plus their passport.

How long can I drive in India on an International Driving Permit?

An International Driving Permit is generally accepted for up to one year, and never beyond its own expiry or that of your home licence. If you are staying longer, apply for an Indian driving licence at the Telangana RTA.

Can an NRI drive in India with an Indian driving licence?

Yes. An Indian licence that is still valid works in India wherever you live. If it has expired while you were abroad, renew it through the Parivahan Sarathi portal and your issuing RTA before you drive.

Is it hard for a foreigner to drive in Hyderabad?

It is demanding at first. Traffic keeps to the left, two-wheelers fill every gap, lane discipline is loose and U-turns replace many right turns. Most visitors find it manageable after a few calm drives at quiet times.
