Is driving in Hyderabad hard? An honest answer for new drivers

Is driving in Hyderabad hard? Yes at first: mixed traffic, U-turns, jams and Old City lanes. What makes it manageable, and a step-by-step plan to get there.

Yes, driving in Hyderabad is hard at first: the traffic is dense and mixed, two-wheelers pass on both sides, many right turns are replaced by U-turns and peak hours can be slow. But it is very learnable, because the main roads are wide and the patterns repeat, and most new drivers get comfortable by building up in stages.

This is an honest look at what makes the city difficult, what makes it easier than it looks, and a plan for getting from nervous to calm. It assumes you are learning with an instructor or a licensed driver beside you.

What makes driving in Hyderabad hard?

The difficulty comes from the mix of traffic and the amount of it, more than from the roads themselves. Here is what new drivers usually mention first.

Mixed traffic

Cars, scooters, motorbikes, autorickshaws, city buses, cabs, delivery bikes, hand carts and pedestrians all share the same road. Each moves at a different speed and follows a different logic. You have to watch many more things at once than in a city where cars dominate.

Two-wheelers everywhere

Two-wheelers pass on your left and your right, fill the gaps at signals and appear suddenly in your mirror. They are the single biggest source of surprises for new car drivers. Our guide to sharing the road with autos, buses and two-wheelers covers how to stay predictable around them.

Autos and buses that stop anywhere

Autorickshaws pick up and drop passengers wherever they are hailed, and buses pull in and out of stops across the lane. Following too closely behind either one is a common beginner mistake.

The U-turn system

On many busy roads the right turn at a junction has been closed. To go right, you continue and turn back at a median opening further on. It works, but it means more merging and more lane changes across fast traffic. Our post on U-turns in Hyderabad explains the technique.

Peak-hour jams and construction

Weekday mornings and evenings are slow, especially around the IT corridor. As of November 2026 Hyderabad Traffic Police advisories for the Jubilee Hills and Banjara Hills flyover works ask drivers to avoid 8.30 to 11 am and 5 to 9 pm. Construction barricades narrow roads for months at a time.

The Old City

Around the Charminar the streets are narrow, many are one-way, shops spill towards the road and crowds on foot are heavy. It is the hardest area for any driver, new or experienced.

Monsoon rain

Heavy showers flood low junctions and underpasses, hide potholes and slow the whole city. A route that is easy in March can be stressful in a September downpour.

What makes it easier than it looks?

Hyderabad has a lot working in a new driver's favour once you know where to look.

  • Wide arterial roads. Many main roads are multi-lane with a central median, so there is no oncoming traffic to worry about and room for other vehicles to pass you.
  • Flyovers. Flyovers carry through traffic over many of the worst junctions, so you can avoid a signal altogether if you choose the right lane early. See our guide to driving on flyovers.
  • The Outer Ring Road. The ORR is an access-controlled expressway around the city with no two-wheelers or autos on the main carriageway. It is often the calmest way between outer areas.
  • Predictable patterns. Traffic keeps left, the same junction layouts repeat across the city, and once you have done one U-turn opening or free left, the next is familiar.
  • Quiet times exist. Early mornings, especially at weekends, are far lighter than rush hour, which gives you a gentle way in.

In the TomTom Traffic Index for 2025, Hyderabad ranked 47th among cities worldwide for congestion, behind Bengaluru and Pune. Our comparison of Hyderabad and Bengaluru traffic looks at the differences.

How long does it take to get comfortable?

It varies a lot from person to person, so treat this as typical experience rather than a rule. Many new drivers feel at ease on a familiar route at a quiet time within a few weeks of regular practice. Busy junctions, flyovers and peak-hour traffic usually take longer, often a few months, before they stop feeling stressful. The Old City can stay demanding for a long time, even for experienced drivers.

Three things speed it up more than anything else: driving regularly rather than once a fortnight, repeating the same routes until they feel boring, and stepping up only when the current stage feels routine. People who already ride a scooter in Hyderabad know the roads, but often need time to unlearn habits; see our tips for moving from a two-wheeler to a car.

If a drive leaves you shaken, go back one stage for a few sessions. Confidence builds from many calm drives, not from one hard one.

A step-by-step plan to get there

  1. Get the paperwork in order. Start with a learner's licence and lessons from a driving school or a licensed driver. Our guide to getting a driving licence in Hyderabad walks through it.
  2. Learn the car in an empty space. Moving off, stopping, slow turns and judging where the car's corners are.
  3. Quiet residential roads at a quiet time. Keeping left, indicating and simple junctions.
  4. A wide, steady road early in the morning. Tank Bund Road or Necklace Road around Hussain Sagar are good choices: few turns, a median and room to settle.
  5. Signals, flyovers and U-turns outside rush hour. Multi-lane roads such as those around HITEC City and Jubilee Hills, planning your lane before each junction.
  6. Light peak traffic. The edge of rush hour on a route you already know well.
  7. The Old City last. Early in the day, slowly, with an experienced driver beside you.

Our guide to driving in Hyderabad for beginners goes into each stage in more detail, and our guide to the best time to drive in Hyderabad helps you pick the quiet slots.

Frequently asked questions

Is driving in Hyderabad hard for beginners?

It is demanding at first, mainly because of dense mixed traffic, two-wheelers on both sides, U-turns in place of right turns and heavy peak-hour jams. Most new drivers find it becomes manageable once they build up from quiet roads at quiet times.

Is Hyderabad easier to drive in than Bengaluru or Mumbai?

Many drivers find Hyderabad's wide arterial roads, flyovers and Outer Ring Road easier than older, narrower city centres, but busy areas still jam badly at peak hours. How hard it feels depends far more on where and when you drive than on the city name.

How long does it take to get comfortable driving in Hyderabad?

There is no fixed time. Many new drivers feel at ease on familiar routes after a few weeks of regular practice, and take a few months before busy junctions, flyovers and the Old City feel routine.

What is the hardest part of driving in Hyderabad?

For most people it is the two-wheelers and autos that fill every gap, combined with junctions where traffic merges from several directions. The narrow, crowded lanes of the Old City around the Charminar are the toughest area of all.
